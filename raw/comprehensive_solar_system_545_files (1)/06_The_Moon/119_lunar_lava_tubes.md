# Lunar Lava Tubes

**Dataset ID:** 119  
**Category:** 06 The Moon

## Overview

Lunar lava tubes are underground tunnels or cave-like structures formed by ancient basaltic lava flows on the [[Moon]].

During periods of lunar volcanism, especially when the lunar maria were forming, low-viscosity basaltic lava flowed across the surface. In some flows, the exposed upper surface cooled and solidified while molten lava continued moving beneath it.

When the eruption weakened or stopped, the remaining lava could drain away, leaving behind a hollow tunnel.

Billions of years later, some of these underground structures may still survive beneath the lunar surface.

Possible lunar lava tubes are scientifically important because they preserve evidence of ancient [[Planetary Volcanism]], provide access to the Moon's subsurface geology, and could potentially provide naturally shielded environments for future lunar exploration.

## What Is a Lava Tube?

A lava tube is a tunnel created inside a flowing lava system.

It is not usually excavated from solid rock after an eruption.

Instead, the tube develops as part of the lava flow itself.

The basic sequence is:

1. Lava erupts onto the surface.
2. The upper surface of the lava cools.
3. A solid crust forms.
4. Hot lava continues flowing underneath the crust.
5. The eruption rate decreases.
6. Molten lava drains from the enclosed pathway.
7. A hollow tunnel remains.

This same general process occurs in basaltic volcanic environments on Earth.

## Why Lava Tubes Form

Lava tubes are especially associated with relatively fluid basaltic lava.

Low-viscosity lava can travel considerable distances before solidifying.

If the outer surface cools while the interior remains molten, the hardened crust acts as insulation.

The insulated lava beneath can remain hot and mobile for longer than an exposed lava flow.

This allows lava to travel farther from its source.

## Lunar Basaltic Volcanism

The Moon was volcanically active for a substantial part of its early geological history.

Large impact basins created during early lunar history were later flooded by basaltic lava.

These eruptions produced the dark volcanic plains called the lunar maria.

Examples include:

- Mare Imbrium
- Mare Serenitatis
- Mare Tranquillitatis
- Oceanus Procellarum

The volcanic systems that produced these plains also generated lava channels, rilles, flow fronts, and potentially extensive lava tubes.

## Lunar Maria

The lunar maria are broad basaltic plains visible from Earth as relatively dark regions.

They formed when magma from the lunar interior reached the surface and flooded low-lying impact basins.

Many eruptions were predominantly effusive.

This means lava flowed across the surface rather than being dominated by explosive fragmentation.

Such basaltic flow environments are favorable for lava-tube formation.

## How Lunar Lava Tubes Form

A lunar lava tube probably began as a basaltic lava flow.

The exposed top of the flow radiated heat into space and cooled.

A solid roof developed while molten lava continued flowing underneath.

The solid roof insulated the moving lava.

When the supply of lava eventually decreased, molten material could drain downslope.

This left an empty or partially empty tunnel beneath the surface.

## Lava Channels and Tubes

Lava channels and lava tubes represent related volcanic transport systems.

An open lava channel transports molten lava along the surface.

If a channel becomes roofed over, it may develop into a lava tube.

Alternatively, tubes can develop internally within sufficiently thick lava flows.

This relationship is important when interpreting lunar volcanic terrain.

## Sinuous Rilles

The Moon contains winding channels known as sinuous rilles.

Many are interpreted as volcanic features produced by flowing lava.

Some may represent:

- open lava channels
- collapsed lava tubes
- combinations of both processes

Their morphology provides evidence that lava was transported across the lunar surface through organized flow systems.

## Hadley Rille

Hadley Rille is one of the best-known lunar sinuous rilles.

Apollo 15 astronauts explored the region near it.

The rille is interpreted primarily as a volcanic channel associated with ancient basaltic lava flows.

Studying features such as Hadley Rille helps scientists understand how lava moved across the lunar surface.

## Collapsed Lava Tubes

A lava-tube roof can collapse.

If collapse occurs along a large section of the tube, it can create an elongated trench.

If only a small section collapses, it may create an isolated opening called a skylight.

These openings can potentially provide access to surviving underground cavities.

## Lunar Skylights

Spacecraft observations have identified deep pits on the Moon that may provide openings into subsurface voids.

Some occur within known volcanic terrain and have been interpreted as possible lava-tube skylights.

A skylight forms when part of a lava-tube roof collapses while surrounding sections remain intact.

The opening can reveal walls, overhangs, or deeper cavities.

## Marius Hills Hole

One of the best-known candidate lava-tube openings is the Marius Hills Hole.

It lies within the volcanically complex Marius Hills region of Oceanus Procellarum.

Images show a steep-sided pit with evidence of an overhanging cavity.

The feature has been investigated as a possible entrance into an ancient lunar lava tube.

## Marius Hills

The Marius Hills region contains numerous volcanic features, including:

- domes
- cones
- lava flows
- sinuous rilles

This volcanic setting strengthens the interpretation that the Marius Hills Hole may be associated with an ancient lava-flow system.

## Mare Tranquillitatis Pit

A deep pit has also been identified in Mare Tranquillitatis.

Observations and later analyses indicate that some lunar pits may connect with larger underground spaces.

Such features are important targets for determining whether substantial lava-tube systems survive beneath the maria.

## Mare Ingenii Pit

Another well-known lunar pit occurs in Mare Ingenii on the lunar far side.

Like other candidate skylights, it may represent roof collapse above a subsurface volcanic cavity.

Comparing multiple pits helps scientists understand whether lava tubes are widespread across lunar volcanic provinces.

## How Large Could Lunar Lava Tubes Be?

The exact dimensions of most lunar lava tubes remain uncertain because they cannot usually be observed directly from orbit.

Models suggest that lunar tubes could potentially be larger than many terrestrial lava tubes.

One important reason is the Moon's lower gravity.

Lower gravity reduces gravitational stresses on cave roofs.

This may allow wider underground cavities to remain mechanically stable.

## Effect of Lunar Gravity

Surface gravity on the Moon is approximately one-sixth of Earth's.

This changes the mechanical behavior of large underground voids.

For similar rock strength and geometry, a lunar cave roof experiences lower gravitational loading than an equivalent structure on Earth.

As a result, very large lava tubes may be mechanically possible.

However, the actual dimensions of individual lunar tubes must be established observationally.

## Why Lava Tubes Can Survive for Billions of Years

The Moon lacks many of the processes that rapidly destroy caves on Earth.

It has:

- no rainfall
- no rivers
- no oceans
- almost no atmosphere
- no active global plate tectonics
- very limited erosion

Consequently, ancient underground volcanic structures can potentially survive for extremely long periods.

Impacts, moonquakes, thermal stresses, and roof collapse can still damage them.

## Temperature Stability

The exposed lunar surface experiences large temperature variations.

Underground environments are more insulated from direct solar heating and radiative cooling.

A sufficiently deep lava tube could therefore maintain much more stable temperatures than the exposed surface.

This is one reason lava tubes are interesting for future exploration.

## Radiation Protection

The Moon lacks a dense atmosphere and global magnetic field capable of providing Earth-like surface protection from radiation.

Astronauts on the lunar surface can be exposed to:

- galactic cosmic rays
- solar energetic particles

Several meters or more of overlying rock could substantially reduce radiation exposure.

A lava tube therefore offers natural shielding.

## Micrometeoroid Protection

The lunar surface is continuously struck by small meteoroids.

A substantial rock roof could protect equipment and habitats from many small impacts.

This provides another potential advantage over exposed surface habitats.

## Protection From Extreme Surface Conditions

A subsurface environment could offer protection from:

- large temperature swings
- radiation
- micrometeoroids
- direct sunlight
- some surface dust movement

These properties make lava tubes attractive targets for future robotic and human exploration.

## Lava Tubes as Potential Habitats

A future lunar base could potentially use a lava tube as a naturally protected environment.

Possible advantages include:

- radiation shielding
- thermal stability
- micrometeoroid protection
- reduced need for massive artificial shielding

However, using a lunar lava tube would present major engineering challenges.

These include:

- difficult access
- unknown structural stability
- uneven terrain
- communications
- power supply
- dust
- emergency evacuation
- mapping unknown cavities

Lava tubes should therefore be viewed as promising possibilities rather than ready-made lunar bases.

## Scientific Value

Lunar lava tubes could preserve geological material that has been shielded from direct surface exposure.

They may contain:

- ancient lava surfaces
- volcanic layering
- mineral deposits
- impact-derived material
- trapped regolith
- records of volcanic processes

Studying these materials could improve understanding of lunar geological history.

## Protection From Space Weathering

Material on the exposed lunar surface experiences space weathering caused by:

- solar wind
- micrometeoroid bombardment
- radiation

Material inside a lava tube would be much better protected.

It may therefore preserve geological surfaces or materials in a less altered condition.

## Lava Tubes and Lunar Volcanic History

The geometry of a lava tube can provide information about the eruption that created it.

Scientists may infer:

- lava-flow direction
- eruption volume
- flow thickness
- cooling behavior
- volcanic duration
- source location

Lava tubes therefore preserve information about ancient lunar magma transport.

## Lunar Lava Tubes and Planetary Volcanism

Lava tubes are an important part of [[Planetary Volcanism]].

They show that volcanic activity is not limited to visible volcanoes.

Magma can create extensive subsurface structures as lava is transported away from eruption vents.

Similar processes may have occurred on other rocky worlds.

## Lava Tubes on Mars

[[Mars]] also contains candidate lava tubes and volcanic skylights.

Large Martian volcanic provinces such as Tharsis contain extensive lava-flow systems.

Like lunar tubes, Martian lava tubes are interesting because they may provide:

- radiation shielding
- stable subsurface environments
- preserved geological records

Mars's atmosphere, gravity, climate history, and water/ice environment make its cave systems different from those on the Moon.

## Earth Versus Lunar Lava Tubes

Earth provides direct examples of how lava tubes form.

Terrestrial lava tubes can be entered, mapped, sampled, and monitored.

The Moon differs because it has:

- lower gravity
- essentially no atmosphere
- no rainfall
- much weaker erosion
- extreme surface temperature cycles

These differences may allow lunar tubes to become larger and survive much longer.

## Lava Tubes Versus Impact Pits

Not every lunar hole or pit is a lava-tube entrance.

Impact craters can also create depressions.

Scientists distinguish candidate volcanic pits using:

- geological setting
- shape
- depth
- wall structure
- surrounding lava terrain
- presence of overhangs
- relationship with rilles or volcanic plains

Context is essential.

## Lava Tubes Versus Rilles

A rille is a long channel or depression.

Some sinuous rilles are volcanic channels.

Some may include collapsed sections of former lava tubes.

However, not every rille is necessarily an intact lava tube.

The terms should therefore not be treated as interchangeable.

## How Scientists Detect Lunar Lava Tubes

Because most lava tubes are underground, scientists must often detect them indirectly.

Several methods are used.

## Orbital Imaging

High-resolution spacecraft cameras can identify:

- pits
- skylights
- collapsed trenches
- overhanging walls
- volcanic channels

Lighting conditions can reveal the geometry of pit walls and floors.

## Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has provided detailed images and measurements of lunar pits and volcanic terrain.

Its observations have been central to investigating candidate lava-tube entrances.

## SELENE/Kaguya

Japan's SELENE, also known as Kaguya, helped identify important lunar pit structures including the Marius Hills Hole.

Its observations contributed significantly to modern interest in lunar lava tubes.

## Radar Sounding

Radar can potentially detect subsurface structures by transmitting radio waves and analyzing reflected signals.

Certain radar observations have been interpreted as evidence consistent with underground voids associated with lunar pits.

Radar is therefore an important technique for future lava-tube exploration.

## Gravity Measurements

A large underground cavity can slightly alter the local gravitational field.

High-resolution gravity measurements may help constrain subsurface structures.

However, detecting individual lava tubes from gravity alone can be difficult.

## Thermal Measurements

Subsurface cavities can respond differently to temperature changes than exposed terrain.

Thermal observations of pits can help investigate whether they connect to deeper spaces.

## Robotic Exploration

Ultimately, direct robotic exploration could provide the strongest evidence about lava-tube interiors.

Possible systems include:

- small rovers
- hopping robots
- tethered probes
- climbing robots
- autonomous drones designed for low-gravity environments

Such missions could map cave geometry and analyze exposed rock.

## Structural Stability

A lava tube is not automatically safe.

Its stability depends on:

- roof thickness
- rock strength
- fracture density
- tube width
- impact history
- seismic activity

Some tubes may contain extensive collapsed sections.

Detailed engineering surveys would be required before human use.

## Moonquakes

The Moon experiences seismic activity known as moonquakes.

These can result from:

- tidal stresses
- thermal contraction
- tectonic processes
- impacts

Seismic shaking could influence the stability of large underground cavities.

## Impacts

Large impacts can fracture or collapse lava-tube roofs.

Smaller impacts may also weaken surrounding rock over geological time.

The preservation of a lava tube therefore depends partly on its depth and geological history.

## Lunar Dust

Lunar regolith consists of fine, abrasive material.

Dust can create problems for:

- mechanical equipment
- seals
- spacesuits
- electronics
- human health

Lava-tube interiors may contain their own deposits of dust or debris.

Subsurface exploration therefore does not eliminate lunar dust challenges.

## Lava Tubes and Water Ice

The best-established major reservoirs of lunar water ice occur in permanently shadowed regions near the poles.

Most known candidate lava-tube pits are associated with volcanic regions rather than these polar cold traps.

A lava tube should therefore not automatically be assumed to contain accessible water ice.

Local thermal conditions, latitude, depth, and volatile transport would need to be evaluated.

## Lava Tubes and Astrobiology

The Moon is not considered a likely location for an indigenous biosphere.

However, lava tubes remain interesting to astrobiology because protected subsurface environments can preserve materials differently from exposed surfaces.

They also provide analog environments for understanding cave exploration on Mars and other worlds.

## Evidence Strength

There is strong evidence that the Moon experienced extensive basaltic volcanism and produced lava channels and tube-forming flow systems.

Several lunar pits are strong candidates for openings into subsurface volcanic cavities.

However, the complete geometry and extent of most proposed lava tubes remain uncertain.

It is important to distinguish:

- observed pits
- inferred cavities
- modeled tube dimensions
- directly mapped underground spaces

## Common Misunderstandings

### Every Lunar Pit Is a Lava Tube

Incorrect.

Some depressions can form through impacts or other collapse processes.

Geological context is required.

### Scientists Have Fully Mapped Huge Lunar Caves

Incorrect.

Candidate pits and subsurface voids have been identified, but most proposed lava tubes have not been directly explored or completely mapped.

### Lunar Lava Tubes Still Contain Molten Lava

Incorrect.

They are remnants of ancient volcanic systems.

The lava that formed them solidified billions of years ago.

### Lava Tubes Are Artificial Structures

Incorrect.

They are natural volcanic landforms.

### Lava Tubes Are Guaranteed Safe for Astronauts

Incorrect.

Their structural stability must be investigated before any human use.

### All Lunar Lava Tubes Contain Water Ice

Incorrect.

Water-ice stability depends on environmental conditions. Lava tubes should not automatically be treated as ice reservoirs.

### The Moon Was Never Volcanically Active

Incorrect.

The lunar maria and numerous volcanic structures provide extensive evidence of ancient volcanism.

## Scientific Importance

Lunar lava tubes connect several important areas of planetary science.

They provide information about:

- [[Planetary Volcanism]]
- ancient lunar lava flows
- magma transport
- lunar thermal evolution
- volcanic landforms
- subsurface geology
- cave stability in low gravity
- future lunar exploration

They may also provide protected locations where geological materials have experienced less space weathering than exposed surfaces.

## Open Questions

Important questions include:

- How many intact lava tubes exist beneath the Moon?
- How large are the largest surviving cavities?
- How deep are they?
- How thick are their roofs?
- How structurally stable are they?
- Which lunar pits connect to extensive underground tunnels?
- How far do individual tubes extend?
- What geological materials are preserved inside?
- Can radar reliably map their geometry?
- Could future robots safely enter them?
- Could some tubes eventually support human infrastructure?
- Do any contain useful volatile resources?

## Summary

Lunar lava tubes are underground volcanic structures created when the surfaces of basaltic lava flows solidified while molten lava continued moving beneath them.

When the lava supply declined and the remaining molten material drained away, hollow tunnels could remain.

These structures are associated with the Moon's ancient volcanic history and the formation of the lunar maria.

Candidate openings such as the Marius Hills Hole and other lunar pits provide evidence that subsurface cavities may still exist.

Because the Moon has low gravity, almost no atmospheric erosion, and little geological recycling, some lava tubes may have survived for billions of years.

They are scientifically valuable records of ancient volcanism and potentially important environments for future exploration because underground rock can provide radiation, thermal, and micrometeoroid protection.

However, the size, geometry, stability, and accessibility of most lunar lava tubes remain uncertain and require further observation and direct exploration.
