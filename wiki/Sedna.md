# Sedna

Sedna is one of the most distant known large bodies in the Solar System. It is a trans-Neptunian object with an extremely elongated orbit and a perihelion far beyond the orbit of [[Neptune]]. Because Neptune cannot strongly perturb Sedna at its current closest approach to the Sun, Sedna is usually classified as a detached object rather than an ordinary member of the [[Scattered Disc]].

Sedna is scientifically important because its unusual orbit may preserve evidence of conditions that existed very early in Solar System history. Its trajectory has been used to investigate the Sun's birth environment, early stellar encounters, the migration of the giant planets, possible distant planetary perturbers, and the structure of the outermost Solar System.

Sedna is closely connected with [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]], [[Scattered Disc]], [[Oort Cloud]], [[Planetary Migration]], [[Orbital Dynamics]], and [[Solar System Formation]].

## Key Points

* Sedna is an extremely distant and unusual [[Trans-Neptunian Objects|Trans-Neptunian Object]] with an elongated orbit extending from roughly 76 AU at perihelion to hundreds of AU at aphelion.
* Its orbital period is approximately 11,000 years.
* Unlike typical [[Scattered Disc]] objects, Sedna never approaches [[Neptune]] closely enough for ordinary present-day planetary encounters to explain its orbit.
* This makes it a detached object and possible member of an inner [[Oort Cloud]] population.
* Several mechanisms may explain its origin, including stellar encounters in the Sun's birth cluster, early [[Planetary Migration]], resonance effects, ancient lost planets, or the hypothetical Planet Nine.
* Sedna is roughly a thousand kilometers across and has a very red, extremely cold surface likely containing ices and radiation-processed organic-rich materials.
* Because its orbit may preserve evidence from the earliest Solar System, Sedna is one of the most important objects for studying the distant boundary between the trans-Neptunian region and the Oort Cloud.
