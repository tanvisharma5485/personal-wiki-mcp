# Charon

Charon is the largest moon of [[Pluto]] and one of the most remarkable satellite systems in the outer Solar System. It is unusually large compared with the body it orbits: Charon's diameter is a little more than half that of Pluto. Because of this unusually high moon-to-primary size ratio, Pluto and Charon are sometimes informally described as a double dwarf-planet system, although Charon is officially classified as a natural satellite.

Charon is an icy, rocky world with a diameter of about 1,212 kilometers. Its surface contains extensive water ice, impact craters, enormous tectonic fractures, mountains, plains, and a distinctive reddish polar region.

The Pluto-Charon system is also dynamically unusual. Pluto and Charon are mutually tidally locked, so each continuously presents approximately the same hemisphere toward the other. Their common center of mass, or barycenter, lies outside Pluto itself.

Charon is scientifically important for understanding [[Natural Satellites]], giant impacts, tidal evolution, [[Planetary Tectonics]], icy-world geology, [[Planetary Differentiation]], and the evolution of [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]].

## Key Points

* Charon is the largest moon of [[Pluto]] and has a diameter of approximately 1,212 kilometers, slightly more than half Pluto's diameter.
* Its unusually large size relative to Pluto creates a binary-like system in which both bodies orbit a barycenter located outside Pluto.
* Pluto and Charon are mutually tidally locked and complete their rotations and mutual orbit in about 6.4 Earth days.
* Charon's surface is dominated by water ice and contains a reddish north polar region thought to form partly from methane escaping Pluto and being chemically processed by radiation.
* The moon has enormous tectonic fractures, mountains, impact craters, and the smooth plains of Vulcan Planitia. These features suggest extensive ancient geological activity and possible [[Cryovolcanism]].
* One leading model proposes that Charon once possessed a subsurface ocean. As the interior cooled and water froze, expansion may have fractured the crust on a global scale.
* Charon probably formed through a giant impact involving the young Pluto and another large icy body. Debris from this event may also have produced Pluto's smaller moons.
* The [[New Horizons]] flyby in 2015 revealed this complex geology and transformed Charon from a distant point of light into one of the best-studied large worlds of the [[Kuiper Belt]].
* Charon therefore provides important evidence about giant impacts, tidal evolution, icy-world geology, internal oceans, [[Planetary Tectonics]], and the formation of satellite systems in the outer Solar System.
