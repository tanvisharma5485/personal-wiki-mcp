# Deimos

Deimos is the smaller and outermost of the two natural satellites of [[Mars]]. The other Martian moon, [[Phobos]], is larger and orbits much closer to the planet.

Deimos is a small, irregularly shaped, dark body with a heavily cratered surface covered by loose regolith. It orbits Mars at a much greater distance than Phobos and takes slightly more than one Martian day to complete an orbit.

Because Deimos lies outside Mars's synchronous-orbit radius, tidal interactions cause it to migrate very slowly outward. This is the opposite of Phobos, which lies inside the synchronous radius and is gradually spiraling toward Mars.

The origin of Deimos remains uncertain. Its primitive-looking surface resembles some asteroids, supporting a capture hypothesis, while its nearly circular and equatorial orbit is easier to explain through formation from debris around early Mars.

Deimos is therefore important for understanding [[Mars]], [[Natural Satellites]], satellite formation, [[Tides]], [[Orbital Dynamics]], impact-generated debris disks, and the early evolution of the Martian system.

## Key Points

* Deimos is the smaller and outer moon of [[Mars]]. It is an irregular body roughly 15 kilometers across at its longest dimension and orbits about 20,000 kilometers above the Martian surface.
* It completes one orbit in approximately 30.3 hours and is tidally locked to Mars.
* Because Deimos orbits outside Mars's synchronous-orbit radius, tidal interactions cause it to move very slowly away from the planet. This contrasts with [[Phobos]], which lies inside synchronous orbit and is gradually spiraling inward.
* Deimos has a dark, heavily cratered surface partly covered by thick regolith that gives it a smoother appearance than Phobos.
* Its origin remains uncertain. Its physical appearance resembles primitive asteroids, but its nearly circular equatorial orbit is more naturally explained if it formed from debris produced by a giant impact on early Mars.
* Future exploration of the Martian moons may determine which explanation is correct and reveal whether Phobos and Deimos are surviving remnants of a once much larger moon-and-ring system.
* Deimos therefore provides valuable information about [[Mars]], moon formation, [[Tides]], [[Orbital Dynamics]], impact evolution, and the long-term history of planetary satellite systems.
