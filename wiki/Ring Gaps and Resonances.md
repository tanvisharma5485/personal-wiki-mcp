# Ring Gaps and Resonances

Ring gaps, waves, and sharp boundaries record the transfer of energy and angular momentum between ring particles and moons through orbital resonances and other gravitational interactions.

## Key Points

* A gap is depleted, not necessarily empty: it may contain diffuse dust, narrow ringlets, low-density material, or embedded moons. Broad low-density regions such as Saturn's Cassini Division can have complex internal structure.
* At a mean-motion resonance, orbital frequencies are close to a ratio of small integers, so repeated long-range forcing accumulates. Resonant torques can move material, excite eccentricity, launch waves, confine edges, and oppose viscous spreading.
* Lindblad resonances launch spiral density waves, while vertical resonances can produce bending waves. Their wavelengths and amplitudes can constrain surface mass density, effective viscosity, moon masses, and planetary gravity.
* The Cassini Division is strongly influenced by the 2:1 resonance with [[Mimas]], but it is neither empty nor explained by a single simple clearing process. Pan helps maintain the Encke Gap, while Daphnis perturbs the Keeler Gap and produces vertical edge waves.
* Shepherd moons maintain narrow boundaries through gravitational exchange of angular momentum rather than physical contact. Embedded moonlets too small to clear a full gap can produce partial, propeller-shaped disturbances.
* Moon-induced wakes are caused by a moon's perturbations; self-gravity wakes instead arise from clustering among [[Ring Particles]]. A resonance may create a wave without an obvious gap, and a gap need not contain a visible moon.
* Some Saturnian waves are excited by the planet's internal oscillations. This ring seismology provides evidence about [[Planetary Interiors]].
* Neptune's Adams-ring arcs require confinement because Keplerian shear would otherwise disperse them; Galatea-related resonant dynamics are important to models, although details remain unsettled. Uranus's narrow, sharp rings likewise indicate dynamical confinement.
