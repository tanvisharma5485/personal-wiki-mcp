# Planetary Volcanism

Planetary volcanism is the movement of molten or partially molten material from the interior of a planet, moon, dwarf planet, or other body toward or onto its surface.

On rocky worlds, volcanism usually involves magma made of molten silicate rock. On icy worlds, similar processes can involve water, ammonia, methane, or other volatile-rich materials and are called [[Cryovolcanism]].

Volcanism is one of the most important ways planetary interiors exchange heat and material with their surfaces. It influences crust formation, atmospheric evolution, tectonics, surface renewal, mineral composition, and potentially [[Planetary Habitability]].

## Key Points

* Melt or mobile volatile-rich material must be generated, remain warm enough to move, and find fractures or other pathways through the lithosphere. Retained formation heat, differentiation, radioactive decay, impacts, and tidal flexing can supply energy; size controls how quickly a world loses it.
* Decompression, added heat, and volatiles that lower rock's melting temperature can generate magma. Composition, temperature, crystals, dissolved gases, gravity, atmosphere, eruption rate, and terrain then control whether eruptions are effusive or explosive and how far material travels.
* Volcanic landforms include shield volcanoes, domes, cones, calderas, fissures, plains, channels, lava tubes, vents, pits, lava lakes, pyroclastic deposits, and cryovolcanic structures. Overlapping flows bury craters and let mapping, crater counts, topography, spectra, and thermal data reconstruct relative histories.
* [[Earth]] couples volcanism to plate boundaries, hotspots, crust production, volatile cycling, climate, and hydrothermal systems. [[Venus]] shows extensive plains, coronae, domes, and evidence consistent with recent or ongoing activity without established Earth-like global plate tectonics.
* Ancient effusive eruptions built [[Mercury]]'s plains and filled basins, while pyroclastic vents reveal volatile-bearing magma. The [[Moon]] preserves basaltic maria, channels, rilles, tubes, and explosive deposits; [[Mars]] preserves Tharsis and Elysium, giant shields, extensive flows, and candidate tubes.
* Olympus Mons is the largest known volcano: a broad Martian shield built by repeated fluid-lava eruptions. Its exceptional scale reflects a long-lived magma source, little plate motion, low gravity, and a strong thick lithosphere; no eruption has been directly observed today.
* [[Io]] is the most volcanically active known world because the Laplace resonance maintains eccentricity and intense tidal heating. Its silicate lava, paterae, lakes, hotspots, and plumes rapidly resurface the moon and supply material to Jupiter's magnetosphere.
* Volcanism transfers heat and chemicals from [[Planetary Interiors]] to surfaces, builds crust, releases atmospheric gases, interacts with [[Planetary Tectonics]], and can create hydrothermal environments relevant to [[Planetary Habitability]].
