# Planetary Rings

Planetary rings are thin, dynamic disks of independently orbiting particles rather than solid sheets. All four [[Giant Planets]] have rings, but their composition, structure, brightness, and means of replenishment differ substantially.

## Key Points

* Ring material ranges from microscopic dust to large blocks and embedded moonlets. Saturn's bright main rings are dominated by water ice; Jupiter's rings are dusty and replenished by impacts on small moons; Uranus has narrow dark rings; and Neptune's faint rings include localized arcs.
* Neighboring particles follow Keplerian orbits, with inner material moving faster than outer material. Repeated low-speed collisions damp random and vertical motion, flattening rings near a planet's equatorial plane while redistributing angular momentum and driving radial spreading.
* Rings can originate through tidal or impact disruption of moons, collisions, impact ejecta from small satellites, active-moon emissions, or material associated with satellite formation. Formation and present-day maintenance are distinct: short-lived dust may require continual replenishment.
* The Roche limit marks an approximate region where planetary tides can disrupt weak bodies or inhibit loose material from assembling into a large moon. Its location depends on the densities and strength of the bodies and is not a perfectly sharp boundary.
* [[Natural Satellites]] shape rings through resonant torques, shepherding, wakes, and direct perturbations. These interactions can produce gaps, sharp edges, waves, narrow ringlets, arcs, and propeller structures while opposing collisional spreading.
* Ring systems exchange material with moons and planets. Material can spread outward and accrete into moonlets, be returned by impacts or disruption, or move inward through collisions, plasma interactions, and electromagnetic transport.
* Composition, mass, contamination, spreading, recycling, and loss rates are used to reconstruct ring histories, but Saturn's ring age and the origins of several individual systems remain uncertain.
* Spacecraft imaging, stellar and radio occultations, spectroscopy, photometry, thermal observations, gravity measurements, and simulations reveal ring structure and particle properties.
