# Cassini-Huygens

Cassini-Huygens was an international robotic mission that transformed scientific understanding of [[Saturn]], its [[Planetary Rings]], atmosphere, magnetosphere, and diverse system of moons. The mission consisted of NASA's Cassini orbiter and the European Space Agency's Huygens probe, with important contributions from the Italian Space Agency.

Cassini became the first spacecraft to orbit Saturn and spent more than thirteen years studying the planet and its surroundings. Huygens separated from Cassini and successfully descended through the atmosphere of [[Titan]] in 2005, becoming the first spacecraft to land on a world in the outer Solar System and the first landing on a moon other than Earth's Moon.

Among the mission's most important discoveries were active water-rich plumes erupting from [[Enceladus]], strong evidence for its global subsurface ocean, hydrocarbon lakes and seas on Titan, detailed structures within Saturn's rings, complex interactions between moons and rings, unusual features on moons such as [[Iapetus]], [[Rhea]], and [[Mimas]], and major insights into Saturn's atmosphere and magnetic environment.

## Key Points

* Cassini-Huygens was an international mission that explored [[Saturn]] and its satellite system from 2004 to 2017 after launching in 1997.
* Cassini became the first spacecraft to orbit Saturn, while Huygens became the first probe to land on [[Titan]].
* The mission revealed Titan's methane lakes, seas, channels, dunes, weather and complex atmosphere. It discovered active water-rich plumes on [[Enceladus]] and provided strong evidence for a global subsurface ocean containing salts, organic compounds and chemical energy sources.
* Cassini investigated Saturn's atmosphere, magnetic field and extraordinarily complex [[Planetary Rings]], while close observations transformed understanding of moons including [[Rhea]], [[Iapetus]] and [[Mimas]].
* During the 2017 Grand Finale, Cassini repeatedly passed between Saturn and its rings before deliberately entering the planet's atmosphere to protect potentially habitable moons from accidental contamination.
* Cassini-Huygens remains one of the most scientifically productive missions in the history of [[Space Exploration]] and established the Saturn system as one of the most important regions for studying planetary evolution, ocean worlds, organic chemistry and potential habitability.
