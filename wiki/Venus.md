# Venus

Venus is an Earth-sized terrestrial planet whose dense atmosphere drives extreme surface conditions.

## Key Points

* Its carbon-dioxide atmosphere and sulfuric-acid clouds produce intense greenhouse heating.
* It rotates slowly and retrograde, has no natural moons, and lacks a strong internally generated global magnetic field.
* Its surface preserves volcanic and tectonic landforms.

## Detailed Knowledge


### Overview

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and one of the four terrestrial, or rocky, planets of the Solar System. It is Earth's closest planetary neighbor in size and overall bulk composition and is often called Earth's "twin" because the two planets have similar diameters, masses, densities, and rocky interiors.

Despite these similarities, Venus is radically different from Earth today. It has an extremely dense carbon-dioxide atmosphere, clouds containing sulfuric acid, crushing surface pressure, almost no surface water, and an extreme greenhouse effect that raises its average surface temperature to about 465°C. This makes Venus the hottest planet in the Solar System, even hotter than Mercury.

Venus rotates extremely slowly and in the opposite direction from most planets. A single rotation takes about 243 Earth days, while one orbit around the Sun takes about 224.7 Earth days. As a result, a Venusian sidereal day is longer than a Venusian year.

Venus has no natural moons and no rings.

Its surface is dominated by volcanic plains, enormous volcanoes, mountains, impact craters, fractures, lava flows, and unusual structures known as coronae and tesserae. Evidence increasingly suggests that Venus may still be geologically active.

### Position in the Solar System

Venus is the second planet outward from the Sun.

The planets nearest it are:

* Mercury toward the Sun.
* Earth farther from the Sun.

The basic order of the planets is:

Mercury → Venus → Earth → Mars → Jupiter → Saturn → Uranus → Neptune.

Venus orbits at an average distance of approximately 108.2 million km from the Sun.

This is about:

0.72 astronomical units.

One astronomical unit is approximately the average distance between Earth and the Sun.

---

### Distance From the Sun

Venus's average distance from the Sun is approximately:

108,200,000 km.

Because Venus's orbit is almost circular, the difference between its nearest and farthest distances from the Sun is relatively small.

Its approximate distance from the Sun varies between:

* Perihelion: About 107.5 million km.
* Aphelion: About 108.9 million km.

---

### Distance From Earth

The distance between Earth and Venus constantly changes as both planets orbit the Sun.

During relatively close approaches, Venus can come within roughly 38–40 million km of Earth, depending on orbital geometry.

At other times, when Venus is on the opposite side of the Sun, the distance can exceed 250 million km.

Because planetary orbits are not perfect circles and are not perfectly aligned, the exact minimum distance differs from one encounter to another.

---

### Size

Venus is almost the same size as Earth.

Its diameter is approximately:

12,104 km.

Earth's diameter is approximately:

12,742 km.

Venus therefore has about:

95% of Earth's diameter.

This similarity is one major reason Venus is often called Earth's twin.

---

### Radius

The mean radius of Venus is approximately:

6,051.8 km.

Earth's mean radius is approximately:

6,371 km.

---

### Surface Area

Venus has a surface area of approximately:

460 million km².

This is about:

90% of Earth's total surface area.

Because Earth's oceans cover most of Earth's surface, the solid surface area of Venus is actually comparable to the total land-plus-ocean area of Earth.

---

### Volume

Venus has a volume approximately:

86% of Earth's volume.

This reflects the planets' very similar sizes.

---

### Mass

Venus has a mass of approximately:

4.87 × 10²⁴ kg.

This is about:

81.5% of Earth's mass.

---

### Density

Venus has an average density of approximately:

5.24 g/cm³.

Earth's density is about:

5.51 g/cm³.

The similar densities suggest that Venus and Earth have broadly similar rocky and metallic compositions.

---

### Gravity

Surface gravity on Venus is approximately:

8.87 m/s².

This is around:

90% of Earth's surface gravity.

A person who weighs 60 kg in terms of Earth-equivalent gravitational weight would feel roughly equivalent to about 54 kg under Venusian surface gravity, although mass itself does not change.

Of course, an unprotected person could not survive on Venus because of the enormous temperature and atmospheric pressure.

---

### Escape Velocity

Venus has an escape velocity of approximately:

10.36 km/s.

Earth's escape velocity is approximately:

11.2 km/s.

The similarity reflects the comparable masses and sizes of the two planets.

---

### Orbit Around the Sun

Venus completes one orbit around the Sun in approximately:

224.7 Earth days.

This period defines one Venusian year.

The orbit is among the most circular planetary orbits in the Solar System.

Its orbital eccentricity is only about:

0.0068.

---

### Venusian Year

One year on Venus lasts approximately:

224.7 Earth days.

Because Venus takes about 243 Earth days to rotate once relative to distant stars, its rotation period is actually longer than its orbital period.

Therefore:

A Venusian sidereal day is longer than a Venusian year.

---

### Orbital Speed

Venus travels around the Sun at an average speed of approximately:

35 km/s.

This corresponds to roughly:

126,000 km/h.

It moves faster around the Sun than Earth because it is closer to the Sun.

---

### Rotation

Venus has one of the strangest rotation patterns of any planet.

It rotates:

* Extremely slowly.
* In the opposite direction from most Solar System planets.

This is called retrograde rotation.

One rotation relative to distant stars takes approximately:

243 Earth days.

---

### Retrograde Rotation

Most planets rotate in the same general direction that they orbit the Sun.

Venus rotates in the opposite direction.

Viewed from above the Solar System's north pole, most planets rotate counterclockwise.

Venus rotates clockwise.

Uranus also has an unusual rotational orientation, although its situation is different because it is tilted almost completely on its side.

---

### Why Venus Rotates Backward

Scientists do not know with certainty why Venus developed retrograde rotation.

Possible explanations involve:

* Giant impacts early in planetary history.
* Gravitational tides caused by the Sun.
* Atmospheric tides.
* Interactions between Venus's mantle and core.
* Long-term changes in rotational dynamics.

Its current slow retrograde rotation may have developed over billions of years rather than resulting from one simple event.

---

### Sidereal Day

A sidereal day measures one complete rotation relative to distant stars.

On Venus, this takes approximately:

243 Earth days.

---

### Solar Day

A solar day measures the time from one local noon to the next.

Because Venus rotates backward while orbiting the Sun, its solar day differs greatly from its sidereal rotation period.

One Venusian solar day lasts approximately:

116.75 Earth days.

Therefore, sunrise-to-sunrise takes about 117 Earth days.

---

### Sunrise and Sunset

Because Venus rotates backward, the Sun would appear to rise approximately in the west and set in the east.

However, the extremely thick cloud cover means someone standing on the surface would not see the Sun clearly as on Earth.

The sky would instead be heavily obscured and illuminated diffusely.

---

### Axial Tilt

Venus is often described as having an axial tilt of about:

177.4°.

This convention reflects its retrograde rotation.

An alternative description treats Venus as rotating backward with a small physical tilt of only a few degrees from an upside-down orientation.

Because of this geometry, Venus does not experience Earth-like seasons.

---

### Seasons

Venus experiences almost no significant seasonal variation.

Reasons include:

* Very small effective axial tilt relative to its upside-down rotation.
* Nearly circular orbit.
* Extremely dense atmosphere that redistributes heat efficiently.

Temperatures are therefore remarkably similar across much of the planet and through its orbital year.

---

### Formation

Venus formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago from material in the protoplanetary disk surrounding the young Sun.

Dust and rocky particles gradually collided and accumulated.

Over time, gravitational attraction caused larger bodies known as planetesimals and planetary embryos to form.

These eventually combined into the terrestrial planets:

* Mercury.
* Venus.
* Earth.
* Mars.

---

### Early Venus

Early Venus may have been substantially different from the planet seen today.

Some scientific models suggest that Venus may once have had:

* A cooler climate.
* Surface liquid water.
* Possibly shallow oceans.
* A more Earth-like environment.

Other models suggest Venus may have become extremely hot much earlier.

Determining whether Venus ever possessed long-lasting oceans remains one of the most important questions in planetary science.

---

### Why Venus and Earth Became Different

Earth and Venus began with similar:

* Sizes.
* Rocky compositions.
* Densities.
* Solar System locations.

Yet today they are dramatically different.

Possible reasons include:

* Venus receives significantly more solar energy.
* Higher temperatures increased evaporation.
* Water vapor strengthened greenhouse warming.
* Water may have been lost into space.
* Carbon dioxide accumulated in the atmosphere.
* Venus lacks Earth's active carbon-cycle system.
* Plate tectonics may be absent or very different.
* The planets may have experienced different volcanic histories.

Understanding this divergence helps scientists understand planetary habitability.

---

### Runaway Greenhouse Effect

Venus is the classic example of a runaway greenhouse environment.

Greenhouse gases allow incoming sunlight to heat the surface while absorbing infrared radiation emitted by the warm surface.

Venus's atmosphere contains enormous amounts of carbon dioxide.

This produces extremely strong greenhouse warming.

Surface temperatures reach approximately:

465°C.

This is hot enough to melt metals such as lead.

---

### Why Venus Is Hotter Than Mercury

Mercury is closer to the Sun than Venus.

However, Venus has a massive carbon-dioxide atmosphere that traps heat extremely efficiently.

Mercury has almost no atmosphere.

Therefore, Mercury loses heat rapidly during nighttime, while Venus retains enormous amounts of heat.

As a result:

Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System.

---

### Surface Temperature

The average surface temperature of Venus is approximately:

465°C.

Depending on location and elevation, temperatures vary somewhat.

However, the thick atmosphere circulates heat efficiently, making the temperature relatively uniform.

Even the night side remains extremely hot.

---

### Temperature at Night

Unlike Mercury, Venus does not cool dramatically during nighttime.

Its massive atmosphere stores and redistributes heat.

Therefore, the difference between daytime and nighttime temperatures is relatively small compared with planets that lack thick atmospheres.

---

### Temperature at the Poles

Venus's polar regions are also extremely hot.

Because its atmosphere redistributes heat efficiently, the temperature difference between equator and poles is smaller than might otherwise be expected.

---

### Atmosphere

Venus possesses the densest atmosphere of any terrestrial planet in the Solar System.

Its atmosphere consists primarily of:

* Carbon dioxide.
* Nitrogen.

It also contains small amounts of:

* Sulfur dioxide.
* Argon.
* Water vapor.
* Carbon monoxide.
* Helium.
* Neon.
* Hydrogen chloride.
* Hydrogen fluoride.
* Other trace gases.

---

### Atmospheric Composition

Approximate composition:

* Carbon dioxide: About 96.5%.
* Nitrogen: About 3.5%.
* Other gases: Trace amounts.

Although nitrogen makes up only a small percentage, Venus's atmosphere is so massive that its total amount of atmospheric nitrogen is substantial.

---

### Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide dominates the atmosphere.

It is responsible for much of Venus's extreme greenhouse warming.

On Earth, much of the planet's carbon is stored in:

* Rocks.
* Oceans.
* Biological systems.

On Venus, an enormous fraction remains in the atmosphere.

---

### Atmospheric Pressure

Surface pressure on Venus is approximately:

92–93 times Earth's sea-level atmospheric pressure.

This is comparable to the pressure experienced roughly:

900 meters underwater in Earth's oceans.

The pressure would rapidly destroy an unprotected spacecraft or human body.

---

### Mass of the Atmosphere

Venus's atmosphere is roughly:

90 times more massive than Earth's atmosphere.

Despite the planets having similar sizes, their atmospheric environments are completely different.

---

### Cloud Layer

Venus is permanently covered by thick clouds.

The main cloud deck lies approximately:

45–70 km above the surface.

These clouds prevent ordinary visible-light telescopes from directly viewing most of the surface.

Radar instruments are therefore especially important for mapping Venus.

---

### Sulfuric Acid Clouds

Venus's clouds consist largely of droplets containing sulfuric acid.

Sulfur dioxide and other sulfur compounds participate in complex atmospheric chemistry.

The droplets do not normally reach the surface as liquid rain because they evaporate in the hot lower atmosphere.

---

### Acid Rain

Sulfuric acid droplets can fall from Venus's clouds.

However, temperatures increase dramatically as the droplets descend.

They evaporate before reaching the ground.

Therefore, Venus does not experience sulfuric-acid rain reaching its surface.

---

### Atmospheric Layers

Venus's atmosphere can broadly be divided into:

* Lower atmosphere.
* Cloud layers.
* Upper atmosphere.
* Thermosphere.
* Exosphere.

Temperature, pressure, winds, and composition vary dramatically with altitude.

---

### Upper Atmosphere

High above the surface, atmospheric conditions become less extreme.

At altitudes around 50–60 km:

* Atmospheric pressure becomes more similar to Earth's surface pressure.
* Temperatures can fall into ranges similar to those found on Earth.

NASA notes that around 50 km altitude, temperatures can fall roughly within 30–70°C in some regions.

This has led scientists to discuss the Venusian cloud layer as a possible location for aerial exploration.

---

### Winds

Although Venus rotates very slowly, its atmosphere moves extremely quickly.

At cloud-top levels, winds can reach:

Hundreds of kilometers per hour.

The atmosphere can circle Venus in only several Earth days.

---

### Atmospheric Super-Rotation

The rapid circulation of Venus's atmosphere is called atmospheric super-rotation.

The upper atmosphere rotates around Venus much faster than the solid planet itself.

Understanding how this circulation is maintained remains an important research problem.

---

### Surface Winds

Near the surface, winds are much slower than those at cloud-top altitude.

However, because the atmosphere is extremely dense, even slow winds can exert substantial force.

---

### Atmospheric Circulation

Venus's global atmospheric circulation includes:

* Equatorial super-rotation.
* Poleward movement.
* Large-scale atmospheric waves.
* Polar vortices.
* Vertical convection.
* Thermal tides.

The Sun strongly affects atmospheric circulation despite the planet's slow rotation.

---

### Polar Vortices

Venus has enormous atmospheric vortices near both poles.

These are large rotating structures in the atmosphere.

The south polar vortex has displayed unusual double-lobed and changing shapes.

---

### Atmospheric Waves

Spacecraft have observed enormous wave patterns in Venus's atmosphere.

One particularly large stationary wave has been associated with the mountainous region Aphrodite Terra.

These observations demonstrate that surface topography can influence atmospheric circulation even through the massive cloud layers.

---

### Lightning

Whether Venus experiences lightning has been debated for decades.

Some spacecraft detected signals that could be associated with electrical activity.

Other observations have challenged this interpretation.

Possible Venusian lightning remains an active area of investigation.

---

### Sky Color

Venus's surface sky would not appear blue like Earth's.

The dense atmosphere, clouds, aerosols, and scattering of sunlight would create an orange, yellowish, or reddish appearance.

Images transmitted by landers have shown an orange-tinted landscape.

---

### Visibility on the Surface

The dense atmosphere and clouds strongly scatter sunlight.

The surface receives diffuse light rather than clear direct sunshine.

Visibility would be limited by haze, although the surface is not completely dark.

---

### Water Vapor

Venus contains only tiny amounts of atmospheric water vapor compared with Earth.

This raises a major question:

Where did Venus's water go?

---

### Water Loss

If Venus once possessed substantial water, much of it may have been lost through a combination of:

* Surface heating.
* Ocean evaporation.
* Water-vapor greenhouse warming.
* Photodissociation in the upper atmosphere.
* Atmospheric escape.

Ultraviolet radiation can split water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen.

Light hydrogen can escape into space relatively easily.

---

### Deuterium Evidence

Venus's atmosphere has an unusually high ratio of deuterium to ordinary hydrogen compared with Earth.

Because ordinary hydrogen escapes more easily than heavier deuterium, this enrichment is considered evidence that Venus lost substantial amounts of water in the past.

---

### Did Venus Have Oceans?

Scientists have not confirmed ancient Venusian oceans.

Climate models show that under some conditions Venus might once have supported liquid water.

Other models suggest that clouds and climate feedbacks may have prevented condensation of large oceans.

Future missions are designed partly to help resolve this question.

---

### Surface

Venus has a rocky surface hidden beneath its clouds.

Radar mapping reveals:

* Vast volcanic plains.
* Mountains.
* Volcanoes.
* Lava flows.
* Rift zones.
* Impact craters.
* Tessera terrain.
* Coronae.
* Fractures.
* Ridges.
* Valleys.

Approximately 80% of the surface is composed of volcanic plains.

---

### Surface Age

The average apparent age of much of Venus's surface is estimated to be hundreds of millions of years.

This is relatively young compared with many surfaces on Mercury, Mars, and the Moon.

The scarcity of very ancient cratered terrain suggests that Venus underwent extensive geological resurfacing.

---

### Global Resurfacing

Scientists once proposed that Venus experienced a catastrophic planet-wide resurfacing event several hundred million years ago.

In this scenario, enormous volcanic eruptions resurfaced much of the planet in a relatively short geological period.

Modern interpretations increasingly consider more complex possibilities involving prolonged or episodic volcanic resurfacing rather than one instantaneous global catastrophe.

---

### Volcanoes

Venus contains more known volcanic structures than any other planet.

Many thousands of volcanic features have been identified.

These include:

* Huge shield volcanoes.
* Small volcanic domes.
* Lava plains.
* Coronae.
* Volcanic channels.

---

### Active Volcanism

Evidence strongly suggests that Venus may still be volcanically active.

Reanalysis of Magellan radar data identified changes in volcanic features interpreted as possible evidence of eruptions during the spacecraft's observing period.

Additional evidence includes:

* Possible recent lava flows.
* Variations in atmospheric sulfur dioxide.
* Thermal anomalies.
* Geologically young volcanic structures.

---

### Maat Mons

Maat Mons is one of the largest and tallest volcanoes on Venus.

It rises several kilometers above surrounding plains.

Magellan radar observations of the region have provided some of the strongest evidence for possible modern volcanic activity.

---

### Sif Mons

Sif Mons is another major Venusian volcano.

It is located in the Eistla Regio region and is associated with extensive volcanic terrain.

---

### Shield Volcanoes

Many Venusian volcanoes resemble shield volcanoes on Earth.

They are formed by relatively fluid lava spreading across large areas.

However, Venusian volcanoes can reach enormous dimensions.

---

### Pancake Domes

Venus contains unusual volcanic structures called pancake domes.

These are:

* Wide.
* Flat.
* Circular.
* Steep-sided.

They may have formed from highly viscous lava.

Some are tens of kilometers across.

---

### Coronae

Coronae are large circular or oval geological structures unique or especially common on Venus.

They may contain:

* Fractures.
* Ridges.
* Lava flows.
* Depressions.

Coronae probably form through interactions between hot rising mantle material and the crust.

Hundreds have been identified.

---

### Arachnoids

Arachnoids are unusual circular geological structures surrounded by networks of fractures.

Their spider-like appearance gave them their name.

They may represent volcanic or tectonic features related to rising magma.

---

### Tessera Terrain

Tesserae are highly deformed highland terrains.

They contain complex patterns of:

* Ridges.
* Fractures.
* Valleys.

Tessera terrain may represent some of the oldest surviving crust on Venus.

Because of this, regions such as Alpha Regio are major targets for future scientific investigation.

---

### Alpha Regio

Alpha Regio is a large tessera highland region.

NASA's DAVINCI mission plans to study Alpha Regio during its atmospheric probe descent because it may contain some of the oldest exposed materials on Venus.

Understanding its rocks could reveal information about Venus's early history and possible ancient water.

---

### Ishtar Terra

Ishtar Terra is one of Venus's two major highland regions.

It lies in the planet's northern hemisphere.

It is roughly comparable in area to Australia.

It contains:

* Lakshmi Planum.
* Maxwell Montes.
* Large mountain systems.

---

### Aphrodite Terra

Aphrodite Terra is Venus's largest highland region.

It lies near the equator.

It is comparable in scale to a large terrestrial continent and contains:

* Mountainous terrain.
* Rift zones.
* Volcanoes.
* Fractured crust.

---

### Maxwell Montes

Maxwell Montes is the highest mountain region on Venus.

Its peaks rise approximately:

11 km above Venus's mean surface level.

This makes it substantially taller relative to the surrounding terrain than many terrestrial mountain ranges.

---

### Lakshmi Planum

Lakshmi Planum is a high plateau within Ishtar Terra.

It is surrounded by mountain ranges and contains volcanic features.

It is several kilometers above Venus's average surface elevation.

---

### Rift Zones

Venus contains extensive rift systems where the crust has been stretched and fractured.

These features may indicate:

* Mantle upwelling.
* Volcanism.
* Tectonic deformation.

Examples include major regions such as Devana Chasma.

---

### Chasmata

Large Venusian valleys or rift systems are often called chasmata.

They can extend hundreds or thousands of kilometers.

Many are associated with volcanic and tectonic processes.

---

### Lava Channels

Venus has exceptionally long lava channels.

One famous example is:

Baltis Vallis.

It extends for more than:

6,000 km.

This makes it one of the longest known channels in the Solar System.

---

### Impact Craters

Venus contains roughly a thousand recognized impact craters.

This is far fewer than Mercury, Mars, or the Moon.

The relatively small number reflects:

* The comparatively young surface.
* Atmospheric destruction of small incoming objects.
* Geological resurfacing.

---

### Atmospheric Protection From Meteoroids

Venus's dense atmosphere causes many small meteoroids to:

* Burn.
* Break apart.
* Explode.

Therefore, small impact craters are relatively rare.

Many crater-forming objects must be large enough to survive passage through the atmosphere.

---

### Crater Distribution

Large impact craters are distributed relatively randomly across Venus.

This observation historically supported the idea that much of the surface has a similar geological age.

---

### Interior Structure

Venus is believed to have an internal structure broadly similar to Earth.

It probably consists of:

* Crust.
* Rocky mantle.
* Metallic core.

However, scientists have much less direct information about Venus's interior than Earth's.

---

### Crust

Venus has a rocky crust dominated largely by basaltic materials.

Its exact thickness varies and remains uncertain.

There is currently no clear evidence for Earth-like global plate tectonics.

---

### Mantle

Below the crust lies a silicate mantle.

Heat from Venus's interior drives:

* Volcanism.
* Mantle convection.
* Crustal deformation.

Understanding how the mantle loses heat is central to understanding Venusian geology.

---

### Core

Venus probably possesses a metallic core composed mainly of:

* Iron.
* Nickel.
* Possibly lighter elements.

Its core may be partly or largely liquid.

However, Venus does not currently generate an Earth-like global magnetic field.

---

### Plate Tectonics

Earth's surface is divided into tectonic plates that move over the mantle.

Venus does not show clear evidence of a modern Earth-style system of global plate tectonics.

Instead, its crust may behave through processes such as:

* Local deformation.
* Mantle plumes.
* Crustal blocks.
* Rifting.
* Volcanic resurfacing.

---

### Crustal Blocks

Radar studies have identified regions where blocks of Venusian crust appear to have moved, rotated, or interacted with surrounding terrain.

These have sometimes been compared with pack ice floating on water.

They may indicate that parts of Venus's crust remain mobile.

---

### Venus Quakes

No confirmed Venusian seismic measurements comparable to Earth's earthquake network currently exist.

However, geological processes should produce seismic activity.

Future landers may eventually detect:

* Volcanic tremors.
* Fault movement.
* Crustal deformation.

---

### Magnetic Field

Venus does not possess a strong internally generated global magnetic field like Earth.

This is surprising because Venus is similar to Earth in:

* Size.
* Mass.
* Internal composition.

---

### Why Venus Has No Global Magnetic Field

A planetary magnetic field usually requires:

* Electrically conductive fluid in the core.
* Internal convection.
* Suitable dynamo conditions.

Possible reasons Venus lacks a strong dynamo include:

* Very slow rotation.
* Different core cooling conditions.
* Limited core convection.
* Differences in mantle heat transfer.

Scientists have not reached a definitive explanation.

---

### Induced Magnetosphere

Although Venus lacks a strong internal magnetic field, it still interacts strongly with the solar wind.

The solar wind interacts with Venus's ionosphere and creates an induced magnetosphere.

This provides limited protection but is very different from Earth's magnetosphere.

---

### Atmospheric Escape

Solar wind and ultraviolet radiation cause particles from Venus's atmosphere to escape into space.

Hydrogen and oxygen escape have been detected.

Studying atmospheric escape helps scientists reconstruct the history of Venusian water.

---

### Ionosphere

Venus has an ionosphere containing electrically charged particles produced by solar radiation.

It interacts directly with the solar wind because Venus lacks a strong intrinsic magnetic shield.

---

### No Moons

Venus has no known natural satellites.

Only Mercury and Venus among the eight planets have no moons.

---

### Why Venus Has No Moon

Scientists do not know exactly why Venus lacks a moon.

Possibilities include:

* It never formed a long-lived moon.
* An ancient moon was lost.
* Gravitational interactions altered satellite orbits.
* A giant impact may have changed the system.

There is currently no confirmed evidence for a former large Venusian moon.

---

### Rings

Venus has no known ring system.

---

### Brightness

Venus is the brightest planet visible from Earth.

It is usually the third-brightest natural object in Earth's sky after:

* The Sun.
* The Moon.

Its apparent magnitude can reach approximately:

−4.7.

This makes it bright enough under certain conditions to cast faint shadows.

---

### Why Venus Is So Bright

Venus appears exceptionally bright because:

* It is relatively close to Earth.
* It is covered by highly reflective clouds.
* Its clouds reflect a large fraction of incoming sunlight.

Its visible geometric albedo is very high.

---

### Morning Star and Evening Star

Venus is commonly known as:

* The Morning Star.
* The Evening Star.

When visible before sunrise, it appears as the Morning Star.

When visible after sunset, it appears as the Evening Star.

It is not actually a star.

---

### Why Venus Never Appears Far From the Sun

Venus orbits closer to the Sun than Earth.

Therefore, from Earth's perspective, Venus always remains relatively near the Sun in the sky.

Its maximum angular separation from the Sun is roughly:

47°.

---

### Phases of Venus

Like the Moon, Venus displays phases.

Observed through a telescope, it can appear:

* Crescent.
* Quarter-like.
* Gibbous.
* Nearly full.

Galileo observed Venus's phases in the early 17th century.

---

### Importance of Galileo's Observations

Galileo's observation of the full set of Venusian phases provided powerful evidence against a simple Earth-centered Ptolemaic model.

The observations showed that Venus orbits the Sun.

This became important evidence supporting heliocentric astronomy.

---

### Apparent Size

Venus appears largest from Earth when relatively close to Earth.

However, at these times it is usually seen as a crescent.

When Venus is farther away, it appears smaller but more fully illuminated.

---

### Greatest Brilliancy

Venus's maximum brightness does not occur when it is closest or completely illuminated.

Its greatest brightness usually occurs when it presents a large crescent or intermediate phase while still relatively close to Earth.

---

### Inferior Conjunction

Inferior conjunction occurs when Venus passes approximately between Earth and the Sun.

Venus is then relatively close to Earth.

Normally it passes above or below the Sun from our viewpoint.

---

### Superior Conjunction

Superior conjunction occurs when Venus lies on the far side of the Sun relative to Earth.

At this time Venus is relatively far away.

---

### Greatest Elongation

Greatest elongation occurs when Venus appears at its greatest angular distance from the Sun.

It can then be especially well positioned for observation before sunrise or after sunset.

---

### Transit of Venus

A transit of Venus occurs when Venus passes directly between Earth and the Sun.

Venus appears as a small dark disk moving across the Sun.

Transits are rare because Venus's orbital plane is tilted relative to Earth's.

---

### Historical Importance of Venus Transits

Astronomers historically used Venus transits to estimate the scale of the Solar System.

By observing the transit from widely separated locations on Earth, scientists could estimate the Earth-Sun distance using parallax.

---

### Transit Cycles

Transits tend to occur in pairs separated by about eight years.

The pairs themselves are separated by more than a century.

The most recent transits occurred in:

* 2004.
* 2012.

The next transit will occur in:

2117.

---

### Surface Color

Venus's actual surface rocks are probably mostly grayish basaltic materials.

However, surface images often appear orange or yellow because light passes through the dense atmosphere.

---

### Surface Lighting

Sunlight at the surface is heavily filtered and scattered by the atmosphere and clouds.

Conditions may resemble a very overcast day, but with unusual coloration and extreme heat.

---

### Radar Mapping

Visible-light cameras cannot easily map Venus's surface from orbit because of the permanent clouds.

Radar waves can penetrate the cloud cover.

Therefore, radar has been essential for studying Venusian topography and geology.

---

* Volcanoes.
* Lava flows.
* Mountains.
* Coronae.
* Craters.

* Rift systems.

Magellan transformed scientific understanding of Venus.

Noble gases such as:

Their abundance and isotopic composition can preserve information about:

### Search for Active Volcanoes

Modern missions will compare radar observations taken at different times.

Scientists can search for:

* New lava flows.
* Changed volcanic vents.
* Surface deformation.
* Earthquake-like movement.
* Thermal anomalies.

Detecting such changes would reveal the present level of Venusian geological activity.

---

### Habitability

The modern surface of Venus is extraordinarily hostile to known life.

Major problems include:

* 465°C temperatures.
* Enormous pressure.
* Extreme dryness.
* Chemical environment.
* Lack of accessible liquid water.

Therefore, known Earth-like organisms could not survive on its surface.

---

### Possible Ancient Habitability

Venus may once have been much more habitable.

If it once contained oceans and moderate temperatures, microbial life could theoretically have developed.

Whether such conditions actually existed and how long they lasted remains unknown.

---

### Possible Life in the Clouds

At approximately 50–60 km altitude:

* Temperature becomes much lower.
* Pressure becomes closer to Earth's surface conditions.

For this reason, scientists have considered whether microbes could theoretically survive in Venusian cloud droplets.

However, major challenges include:

* Sulfuric acid.
* Extreme dryness.
* Limited nutrients.
* Uncertain chemistry.

There is currently no confirmed evidence of life on Venus.

---

### Phosphine Controversy

In 2020, researchers reported possible detection of phosphine in Venus's atmosphere.

On Earth, phosphine can be associated with biological or industrial processes.

The claim generated intense interest.

Subsequent analyses questioned:

* The strength of the signal.
* Data processing.
* Atmospheric abundance.
* Alternative chemical explanations.

Phosphine is therefore not accepted as evidence for Venusian life.

---

### Sulfur Chemistry

Venus's clouds contain complex sulfur chemistry.

Important compounds include:

* Sulfur dioxide.
* Sulfuric acid.
* Sulfur-bearing aerosols.

Studying sulfur chemistry helps scientists understand:

* Volcanism.
* Atmospheric circulation.
* Cloud formation.
* Climate evolution.

---

### Unknown Ultraviolet Absorber

Venus's clouds contain a substance that absorbs ultraviolet light strongly.

Its exact identity remains uncertain.

Possible candidates involve:

* Sulfur compounds.
* Iron-bearing particles.
* Other atmospheric chemicals.

Determining its nature is an important Venus research problem.

### Why Venus Is Called Earth's Twin

Venus and Earth have similar:

* Diameter.
* Mass.
* Gravity.
* Density.
* Rocky composition.
* Internal structure.

However, their climates are radically different.

For this reason Venus is also sometimes informally called:

Earth's evil twin.

---

### Venus Compared With Mercury

Venus is:

* Larger.
* More massive.
* Much more atmospheric.
* Hotter on average.

Mercury is closer to the Sun but lacks Venus's greenhouse atmosphere.

Therefore, Venus maintains a higher average surface temperature.

---

### Venus Compared With Mars

Venus and Mars represent two very different outcomes for terrestrial planets.

Venus has:

* Thick atmosphere.
* Extreme greenhouse warming.
* Very high pressure.

Mars has:

* Thin atmosphere.
* Cold temperatures.
* Low pressure.

Earth lies between these climate extremes.

---

### Venus Compared With Jupiter

Venus is a rocky terrestrial planet.

Jupiter is a gas giant.

Jupiter is enormously larger and consists primarily of:

* Hydrogen.
* Helium.

Venus has a solid rocky surface, while Jupiter has no comparable solid surface accessible beneath its deep atmosphere.

---

### Venus Compared With the Moon

Venus is much larger and more massive than Earth's Moon.

Unlike the Moon, Venus has:

* A massive atmosphere.
* Extensive volcanism.
* Strong greenhouse warming.

The Moon has almost no atmosphere and experiences enormous day-night temperature variation.

---

### Venus Compared With Titan

Both Venus and Titan have dense atmospheres.

However:

Venus's atmosphere is mainly carbon dioxide and extremely hot.

Titan's atmosphere is mainly nitrogen and extremely cold.

Titan has lakes of liquid methane and ethane.

Venus has no stable surface liquid.

---

### Greenhouse Effect Compared With Earth

Earth naturally has a greenhouse effect that keeps the planet warm enough for liquid water.

Venus's greenhouse effect is vastly stronger.

This comparison helps scientists study how atmospheric composition controls planetary climate.

---

### Carbon Cycle

Earth regulates atmospheric carbon dioxide through processes involving:

* Oceans.
* Weathering.
* Plate tectonics.
* Carbonate rocks.
* Living organisms.

Venus lacks a known Earth-like carbon cycle.

Without oceans and active global plate tectonics, carbon dioxide can remain concentrated in the atmosphere.

---

### Why Venus Is Important for Climate Science

Venus provides an extreme natural example of greenhouse warming.

Studying it helps scientists understand:

* Climate feedbacks.
* Atmospheric evolution.
* Water loss.
* Planetary habitability.

However, Venus's runaway greenhouse should not be treated as a simple direct prediction of near-term climate change on modern Earth because the two planets have very different conditions and evolutionary histories.

---

### Importance for Exoplanet Science

Many rocky exoplanets discovered around other stars may resemble:

* Earth.
* Venus.
* Something between them.

Understanding Venus helps scientists determine whether an Earth-sized exoplanet is necessarily Earth-like.

A planet's:

* Atmosphere.
* Water inventory.
* Stellar radiation.
* Geological history.

can produce very different outcomes.

---

### Venus Zone

Scientists sometimes discuss a "Venus Zone."

This refers to a region around a star where rocky planets may be more likely to experience Venus-like greenhouse conditions than Earth-like habitability.

---

### Observing Venus From Earth

Venus is easy to observe without a telescope.

It usually appears as an exceptionally bright white point.

Best viewing occurs:

* Shortly after sunset.
* Shortly before sunrise.

---

### Can Venus Be Seen During Daylight?

Under favorable conditions, Venus is bright enough to be seen with the naked eye during daylight.

However, finding it can be difficult and observers must never look near the Sun without proper safety precautions.

---

### Why Venus Appears White

Venus appears bright white from space because its upper cloud layer reflects large amounts of sunlight.

Ultraviolet observations reveal cloud patterns invisible to ordinary human vision.

---

### No Surface View From Earth

Optical telescopes cannot directly photograph Venus's surface through its clouds.

Radar observations are required for detailed surface mapping.

---

### Human Exploration Challenges

Sending humans to the surface of Venus would be enormously difficult.

Hazards include:

* Extreme heat.
* Crushing pressure.
* Acidic atmosphere.
* Long travel distance.
* Difficult surface operations.
* Rapid equipment failure.

---

### Human Survival on the Surface

An unprotected human could not survive on Venus.

The atmosphere is:

* Unbreathable.
* Extremely hot.
* Extremely high-pressure.

A conventional spacesuit would not be sufficient.

A heavily protected pressure vessel with active cooling would be required.

---

### Floating Habitats

Because conditions at about 50–60 km altitude are much more moderate, scientists and engineers have proposed conceptual floating habitats.

At these altitudes:

* Pressure can be near Earth-like levels.
* Temperature is much lower.
* Carbon-dioxide atmosphere provides buoyancy for nitrogen-oxygen-filled structures.

These ideas remain conceptual.

---

### Why Air Could Float on Venus

In Venus's carbon-dioxide atmosphere, ordinary breathable air containing nitrogen and oxygen is less dense than the surrounding gas.

Therefore, a sealed breathable-air habitat could theoretically act as a lifting gas.

---

### Surface Colonization

Permanent surface settlements are far less practical than atmospheric habitats with current or near-future technology.

A surface base would require extreme protection from:

* Heat.
* Pressure.
* Corrosive conditions.

---

### Terraforming Venus

Terraforming Venus would be extraordinarily difficult.

Major challenges include:

* Removing or storing enormous quantities of carbon dioxide.
* Lowering the surface temperature by hundreds of degrees.
* Dealing with atmospheric pressure.
* Introducing and retaining water.
* Managing the slow rotation.
* Creating long-term climate stability.

No existing technology could terraform Venus.

---

### Possible Resource Use

Future robotic exploration could potentially investigate:

* Atmospheric carbon dioxide.
* Nitrogen.
* Sulfur compounds.
* Surface minerals.

However, practical resource extraction remains speculative.

---

### Solar Energy

Venus receives substantially more solar energy than Earth because it is closer to the Sun.

Above its clouds, solar power could theoretically be extremely productive.

At the surface, however, thick clouds reduce direct sunlight.

---

### Communication Delay

Radio signals travel at the speed of light.

Depending on the positions of Earth and Venus, one-way communication delays can range from only a few minutes to more than ten minutes.

Real-time remote control from Earth is therefore impossible.

Robotic spacecraft need significant autonomy.

---

### Travel Time

Travel time between Earth and Venus varies according to trajectory and mission design.

Typical spacecraft transfers can take:

Several months.

Some flyby trajectories can differ substantially.

---

### Gravity Assist

Venus is often used for gravitational assists.

Spacecraft can fly past Venus to change:

* Velocity.
* Direction.
* Orbital energy.

Missions such as:

* Mariner 10.
* Parker Solar Probe.
* BepiColombo.

have used Venus gravity assists.

---

### Could Venus Become Tidally Locked?

Venus is not tidally locked to the Sun.

Although it rotates extremely slowly, one side does not permanently face the Sun.

Its atmospheric and solar gravitational tides influence its rotation.

---

### Relation Between Venus and Earth Orbits

Venus completes about:

13 orbits

in approximately the same amount of time Earth completes:

8 orbits.

This near 13:8 orbital relationship creates repeating patterns in the relative geometry of the planets.

---

### Five-Petal Pattern

Successive inferior conjunctions between Earth and Venus occur at locations that form an approximate five-fold pattern when plotted over several years.

This geometric pattern is sometimes described as a pentagram or flower-like pattern.

It results from the near resonance between the planets' orbital periods.

---

### Surface Coordinates

Like Earth and other planets, Venusian surface locations are described using:

* Latitude.
* Longitude.

Radar maps allow scientists to identify and compare geological formations globally.

---

### Venusian Elevation

Because Venus has no sea level, scientists define a reference surface using a standard planetary radius.

Elevations are measured relative to this reference level.

---

### Highland Regions

Major elevated regions include:

* Ishtar Terra.
* Aphrodite Terra.
* Beta Regio.
* Alpha Regio.

These regions contain important volcanic and tectonic features.

---

### Beta Regio

Beta Regio is a prominent volcanic highland region.

It contains large volcanoes including:

* Rhea Mons.
* Theia Mons.

It shows evidence of extensive rifting and mantle upwelling.

---

### Atla Regio

Atla Regio is a volcanic region containing several major volcanoes.

It has been considered a possible location of recent geological activity.

---

### Ganiki Chasma

Ganiki Chasma is part of a volcanic and tectonic region associated with possible young lava flows.

---

### Phoebe Regio

Phoebe Regio is another elevated region shaped by volcanic and tectonic processes.

---

### Lowlands

Large lowland plains occupy much of Venus.

These areas are often covered by extensive volcanic lava deposits.

---

### Surface Reflectivity

Radar images show differences in surface reflectivity.

Bright radar areas may indicate:

* Rough terrain.
* Steep slopes.
* Certain mineral compositions.

Dark areas may indicate:

* Smooth lava flows.
* Fine material.
* Different rock properties.

---

### High-Altitude Radar Brightness

Some high mountains on Venus appear unusually bright in radar observations.

Scientists have proposed that unusual metallic or mineral compounds may condense at cooler high-altitude locations, creating a type of radar-reflective "frost."

Its exact composition remains uncertain.

---

### Snow on Venus

Venus does not have ordinary water snow on its mountains.

The term "metallic snow" has sometimes been used for possible mineral condensation on high-elevation terrain.

This remains an area of investigation.

---

### Surface Chemistry

Measurements from Soviet landers suggest that many Venusian plains are composed of basalt-like volcanic rock.

However, large portions of Venus have never been directly sampled.

Future missions may reveal more diverse compositions.

---

### Granite Question

If ancient tessera terrain contains granite-like rocks, it could imply that Venus once had:

* Significant water.
* Complex crustal differentiation.

Because granite formation on Earth is strongly associated with water-rich geological processes, detecting such rocks would have major implications.

This remains unconfirmed.

---

### Weathering

Venus's atmosphere may chemically react with surface rocks.

Possible processes include reactions involving:

* Carbon dioxide.
* Sulfur gases.
* High temperatures.

These reactions could alter minerals over geological time.

---

### Erosion

Venus has no rivers or oceans today.

Therefore, water-driven erosion like that on Earth is absent.

However, erosion and surface modification can occur through:

* Chemical weathering.
* Volcanism.
* Atmospheric processes.
* Impacts.
* Mass movement.

---

### Dust

Unlike Mars, Venus does not appear to experience enormous global dust storms.

Its dense atmosphere and surface conditions affect particles differently.

---

### Surface Wind Erosion

Although near-surface winds are relatively slow, the atmosphere is extremely dense.

This means winds may still move fine particles and modify the landscape.

Some radar features suggest possible wind-related deposits.

---

### Dunes

Some Venusian radar images show structures interpreted as possible dune fields or wind-shaped deposits.

Their detailed nature remains uncertain.

---

### Length of Day Compared With Year

Venus has one of the Solar System's most unusual time relationships.

Sidereal rotation:

About 243 Earth days.

Year:

About 224.7 Earth days.

Therefore, its rotation period is longer than its year.

However, because of retrograde rotation, the solar day is approximately 116.75 Earth days.

---

### Number of Sunrises Per Venusian Year

Because the solar day lasts about 116.75 Earth days while the year lasts 224.7 Earth days, Venus experiences fewer than two complete solar days per Venusian year.

---

### No Conventional Seasons

The combination of:

* Nearly circular orbit.
* Small effective seasonal tilt.
* Dense atmosphere.

means Venus has almost no Earth-like seasonal cycle.

---

### Solar Radiation

Venus receives roughly:

1.9 times as much solar energy per unit area as Earth at the top of its atmosphere.

Much of that sunlight is reflected by its bright clouds.

---

### Albedo

Venus has one of the highest planetary albedos in the Solar System.

Its bright sulfuric-acid clouds reflect a large proportion of incoming sunlight.

Despite reflecting so much sunlight, the energy that is absorbed becomes strongly trapped by the greenhouse atmosphere.

---

### Energy Balance

Venus's extreme climate results from interaction between:

* Incoming solar radiation.
* Cloud reflection.
* Atmospheric absorption.
* Infrared trapping.
* Atmospheric circulation.

---

### Surface Heat Distribution

The dense atmosphere distributes heat efficiently.

Therefore:

* Equator-to-pole differences are relatively small.
* Day-night temperature differences are small.

Elevation produces a more noticeable temperature difference because higher locations have lower atmospheric pressure and are somewhat cooler.

---

### Maxwell Montes Temperature

Mountain peaks such as Maxwell Montes are cooler than lowland areas because temperature decreases with altitude.

However, they remain extremely hot compared with Earth's surface.

---

### Sound on Venus

Unlike the Moon, sound can travel through Venus's atmosphere.

Because the atmosphere is extremely dense and dominated by carbon dioxide, sound would propagate differently than on Earth.

Its speed and tone characteristics would differ substantially.

---

### Falling Objects on Venus

An object falling through Venus's dense atmosphere experiences enormous aerodynamic drag.

The atmosphere can slow falling objects significantly.

This is why descent probes can use atmospheric drag and parachutes before reaching lower altitudes.

---

### Aircraft on Venus

Dense atmospheric conditions could make flight comparatively efficient at some altitudes.

Future Venus missions may use:

* Balloons.
* Aerobots.
* Airships.
* Possibly aircraft.

---

### Pressure at Cloud Altitude

At roughly 50–55 km altitude, atmospheric pressure can be comparable to pressure at Earth's surface.

This makes the cloud layer far more accessible than Venus's ground level.

---

### Temperature at Cloud Altitude

Temperatures in parts of the middle cloud layer can fall within ranges compatible with terrestrial equipment and potentially some forms of microbial life.

NASA notes a temperature range of approximately 30–70°C around 50 km altitude.

---

### Acid Challenge for Cloud Missions

Despite moderate temperature and pressure, Venusian clouds are chemically hostile because of concentrated sulfuric acid droplets.

Aerial spacecraft would require corrosion-resistant materials.

---

### Radiation at Cloud Altitudes

Venus lacks Earth's magnetic field but its thick atmosphere provides substantial shielding from many types of cosmic radiation.

At certain cloud altitudes, atmospheric radiation protection could be better than on the surfaces of Mars or the Moon.

---

### Possible Human Cloud Habitats

Conceptual studies have proposed high-altitude habitats where:

* Temperature is moderate.
* Pressure is manageable.
* Atmospheric density provides buoyancy.
* Radiation exposure may be lower than on airless worlds.

However, such settlements remain purely conceptual and face major engineering challenges.

---

### Importance of Venus for Understanding Earth

Venus demonstrates that planets with similar initial sizes and compositions can evolve in drastically different directions.

Understanding Venus can help answer:

* Why does Earth have oceans?
* Why does Earth maintain plate tectonics?
* Why does Venus have extreme greenhouse warming?
* How long can rocky planets remain habitable?
* What determines whether an Earth-sized planet becomes Earth-like or Venus-like?

### Importance for Studying Habitability

A planet's size alone cannot determine habitability.

Venus demonstrates the importance of:

* Stellar distance.
* Atmospheric chemistry.
* Water inventory.
* Geology.
* Magnetic evolution.
* Carbon cycling.
* Cloud feedbacks.

---

### Importance for Exoplanets

Many discovered exoplanets are close in size to Earth but orbit closer to their stars.

Some may be more accurately considered:

"Exo-Venuses"

rather than Earth analogues.

Understanding Venus is therefore essential for interpreting potentially habitable exoplanets.

### Common Misconceptions

#### Venus Is the Closest Planet to the Sun

False.

Mercury is closest.

Venus is second.

#### Mercury Is the Hottest Planet

False.

Venus is hotter because of its powerful greenhouse effect.

#### Venus Is Exactly the Same Size as Earth

False.

Venus is slightly smaller.

#### Venus Has No Atmosphere

False.

Venus has an extremely massive atmosphere.

#### Venus Is Covered in Sulfuric Acid Oceans

False.

Sulfuric acid occurs mainly as droplets in the clouds.

The surface has no sulfuric-acid oceans.

#### It Rains Sulfuric Acid on the Ground

False.

Sulfuric acid droplets evaporate before reaching the extremely hot surface.

#### Venus Is Always a Morning Star

False.

It alternates between prominent morning and evening appearances.

#### Venus Is a Star

False.

It is a planet reflecting sunlight.

#### Venus Is Earth's Closest Planet at All Times

False.

The identity of the nearest planet changes with orbital positions.

#### Venus Has Oceans Today

False.

There are no known stable liquid-water oceans on its present surface.

#### Venus Has Confirmed Life

False.

No life has been discovered on Venus.

* Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System.
* Its surface temperature is around 465°C.
* Its atmosphere is about 96.5% carbon dioxide.
* Surface pressure is roughly 92–93 times Earth's.
* Venus rotates backward compared with most planets.
* Its sidereal day is longer than its year.
* Venus has no moons.
* Venus has no rings.
* It is almost the same size as Earth.
* Its gravity is about 90% of Earth's.
* It is the brightest planet in Earth's sky.
* Venus displays phases like the Moon.
* Galileo's observations of its phases helped support heliocentric astronomy.
* Its clouds contain sulfuric acid.
* The planet contains thousands of volcanic structures.
* Evidence suggests some volcanic activity may still be occurring.
* Much of its surface is covered by volcanic plains.
* Radar is required to map the surface through its clouds.
* Soviet spacecraft achieved the first successful landings on Venus.
* Venera probes returned the first photographs from another planet's surface.
* NASA, ESA, and other agencies are preparing a new generation of Venus missions.
* Venus may once have been substantially more Earth-like.
* Understanding Venus is crucial for understanding terrestrial planets and exoplanets.

### Scientific Importance

Venus is one of the most important planets for understanding terrestrial planetary evolution.

It represents an extraordinary natural experiment.

Earth and Venus formed:

* At similar times.
* From similar rocky materials.
* With similar sizes.

Yet one developed oceans and conditions suitable for life while the other developed:

* Extreme greenhouse warming.
* Crushing atmospheric pressure.
* Near-total surface dryness.

Understanding why these two neighboring planets diverged may help scientists determine how common habitable worlds are throughout the universe.

---

### Future Importance

The coming generations of Venus missions will attempt to determine:

* Whether ancient Venus had oceans.
* How quickly it lost its water.
* Whether volcanoes remain active.
* What its oldest crust is made of.
* How its atmosphere evolved.
* How its core and mantle behave.
* Why its climate became so extreme.

NASA's DAVINCI and VERITAS and ESA's EnVision are designed to provide major new information about these questions.

### Summary

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and Earth's closest planetary sibling in terms of size, mass, density, gravity, and rocky composition.

It has a diameter of approximately 12,104 km, about 95% of Earth's diameter, and a mass about 81.5% that of Earth.

Despite these similarities, Venus has evolved into one of the most hostile environments in the Solar System.

Its atmosphere consists mainly of carbon dioxide and produces surface pressure roughly 92–93 times that of Earth. Thick sulfuric-acid clouds surround the planet, and an extreme greenhouse effect keeps the surface near 465°C, making Venus hotter than Mercury.

Venus rotates extremely slowly and in the opposite direction from most planets. Its sidereal rotation takes approximately 243 Earth days, longer than its 224.7-day year, while one solar day lasts approximately 116.75 Earth days.

The planet has no moons and no rings.

Beneath its permanent cloud cover lies a geologically complex world containing enormous volcanic plains, thousands of volcanoes, mountains, rift valleys, lava channels, coronae, tessera terrain, and approximately a thousand known impact craters.

Major regions include Aphrodite Terra, Ishtar Terra, Alpha Regio, Beta Regio, Lakshmi Planum, and Maxwell Montes.

Venus may still be volcanically and tectonically active.

Scientists believe Venus may once have been significantly cooler and possibly capable of supporting surface liquid water, although the existence and duration of ancient oceans remain uncertain. Over time, intense greenhouse warming and atmospheric evolution transformed the planet into its current state.

Venus has been explored by many spacecraft, including Mariner missions, Soviet Venera and Vega probes, Pioneer Venus, Magellan, Venus Express, and Akatsuki. Soviet Venera missions achieved the first successful atmospheric measurements, landings, and surface photographs from another planet.

Future missions including NASA's DAVINCI and VERITAS and ESA's EnVision are intended to investigate the atmosphere, surface, interior, geological activity, and possible ancient habitability of Venus.

Because Venus and Earth began with many similarities but evolved so differently, Venus is essential for understanding climate evolution, rocky planets, planetary habitability, atmospheric loss, volcanism, and Earth-sized exoplanets throughout the universe.
