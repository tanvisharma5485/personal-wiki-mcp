## Key Points

* The solar cycle is an approximately 11-year variation in the Sun's magnetic activity.
* It progresses broadly through:
* Solar minimum
* Rising activity
* Solar maximum
* Declining activity
* Next solar minimum
* Sunspot numbers provide the most familiar indicator of the cycle.
* However, the cycle also changes:
* Magnetic fields
* Coronal structure
* Solar flares
* CMEs
* Solar wind
* Energetic particles
* Solar irradiance
* Space weather
* Around solar maximum, the Sun's global magnetic polarity reverses.
* Because two approximately 11-year cycles are required to restore the original magnetic orientation, the complete magnetic cycle lasts about:
* 22 years
* The solar cycle is generated by the solar dynamo involving:
* Differential rotation
* Convection
* Magnetic flux transport
* Meridional circulation
* Sunspots migrate toward the equator as each cycle progresses, producing the:
* Butterfly diagram
* Solar cycles vary substantially in:
* Strength
* Duration
* Shape
* Hemispheric behavior
* Longer-term variations include grand minima such as the:
* Maunder Minimum
* Understanding the solar cycle is essential for studying solar physics, predicting space-weather risk, protecting technological systems, planning human spaceflight, and interpreting magnetic activity on other stars.
