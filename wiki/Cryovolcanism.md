# Cryovolcanism

Cryovolcanism is volcanic-like activity in which cold planetary bodies erupt or move volatile-rich materials rather than ordinary molten silicate rock.

The erupted material may include water, water ice, brines, ammonia-bearing mixtures, methane-related compounds, nitrogen-rich material, or other volatiles depending on the world.

The word combines:

Cryo = cold

Volcanism = movement of interior material toward the surface

Cryovolcanism is most important on icy moons, dwarf planets, and other cold bodies in the outer Solar System.

It is closely related to [[Planetary Volcanism]], [[Planetary Interiors]], [[Planetary Tectonics]], [[Tides]], and [[Water in the Solar System]].

## Key Points

* Cryovolcanism is volcanic-like activity involving volatile-rich materials instead of ordinary molten rock.
* Internal mobility can be sustained by tidal, radiogenic, or retained formation heat; freezing-induced pressure can also force liquid outward. Ammonia can lower freezing temperatures, while salts change freezing behavior, density, and viscosity.
* Fractures link subsurface reservoirs to the surface. Eruptions may create jets, plumes, flows, domes, smooth terrain, or frozen deposits, but a plume alone does not prove deep cryovolcanism because sublimation or solar heating can also drive jets.
* [[Enceladus]] provides the clearest directly observed example: water vapor, ice grains, salts, and organic compounds escape through warm south-polar fractures, and some particles feed Saturn's E ring. Its plume samples material connected to a subsurface ocean and evidence of water-rock interaction.
* [[Europa]] has an ocean and active ice shell, but its reported plumes are less certain than Enceladus's. Triton's observed plumes may be substantially solar-driven; Titan's Doom Mons and Pluto's Wright Mons are candidates rather than directly observed active volcanoes.
* [[Ceres]] preserves strong geological evidence: Ahuna Mons is widely interpreted as a cryovolcanic dome, while Occator's salt-rich deposits record brine movement. Ganymede mainly records ancient icy resurfacing rather than clear modern eruptions.
* Evidence must combine morphology, composition, thermal behavior, geology, and—where possible—direct plume sampling. Few craters can indicate resurfacing but do not identify its cause by themselves.
* Because cryovolcanism can transport hidden-ocean material to accessible surfaces or space, it is central to studying [[Water in the Solar System]], ocean worlds, and [[Planetary Habitability]] without implying that life exists.
