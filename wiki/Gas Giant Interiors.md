# Gas Giant Interiors

The interiors of [[Jupiter]] and [[Saturn]] are hydrogen-helium-dominated, high-pressure fluid systems with electrically conducting hydrogen, distributed heavy elements, and no conventional solid surface.

## Key Points

* Atmospheric molecular hydrogen becomes progressively denser with depth and eventually enters a metallic, electrically conducting state. Convection in this region supports planetary dynamos, especially Jupiter's powerful [[Planetary Magnetism|magnetic field]].
* Modern models do not require sharply separated shells or compact solid cores. Jupiter may have a dilute core in which heavy-element abundance rises gradually toward the center; possible causes include primordial gradients, core erosion, outward mixing, or a giant impact, but the history is uncertain.
* Saturn may likewise have a broad compositionally stratified central region. Ring seismology uses waves driven in [[Planetary Rings|Saturn's rings]] by internal oscillations to constrain that deep structure.
* Helium can become less soluble in metallic hydrogen and sink as droplets. This "helium rain" redistributes helium and releases gravitational energy, helping explain Saturn's excess luminosity; separation may also occur in Jupiter.
* Residual formation heat and slow gravitational contraction also power internal emission. Composition gradients can inhibit convection, changing mixing, cooling, oscillations, and dynamo behavior.
* Interiors are inferred rather than directly observed. Key constraints include gravity harmonics, magnetic fields, thermal emission, rotation and oblateness, atmospheric composition, high-pressure experiments, equations of state, and numerical models. Juno greatly improved Jupiter constraints, while Cassini gravity data and ring seismology refined Saturn models.
* Different combinations of composition, temperature, equations of state, layer boundaries, and heavy-element distribution can match similar observations, so current interior solutions are not unique.
* Interior structure connects [[Planet Formation]] to present-day properties: the amount and distribution of heavy material can record gas accretion, planetesimal dissolution, mixing, impacts, and [[Planetary Migration|migration]].
