# Atmospheric Escape

Atmospheric escape is the loss of gas from a planet or moon into space.

An atmosphere is held by gravity, but atmospheric particles are constantly moving. Some particles can gain enough energy to overcome a world's gravitational pull and escape permanently.

Atmospheric escape can strongly change a planet's climate, surface pressure, water inventory, atmospheric chemistry, and long-term [[Planetary Habitability]].

It is especially important for smaller worlds such as [[Mars]], bodies exposed to strong stellar radiation, and planets orbiting close to their stars.

Atmospheric escape is closely connected with [[Planetary Atmospheres]], [[Solar Wind]], [[Space Weather]], [[Planetary Magnetism]], [[Water in the Solar System]], and [[Planetary Evolution]].

## Key Points

* Atmospheric escape is the permanent loss of gases from a planetary atmosphere into space.
* Light particles such as hydrogen escape most easily, but many mechanisms can remove heavier species as well.
* Thermal processes include Jeans escape and hydrodynamic escape. Non-thermal processes include sputtering, ion pickup, photochemical escape, charge exchange, and plasma-driven loss.
* Atmospheric escape has played a major role in the history of [[Mars]], contributed to the water evolution of [[Venus]], and continuously occurs on [[Earth]].
* It is especially powerful on young planets exposed to intense ultraviolet and X-ray radiation.
* Among [[Exoplanets]], atmospheric escape can transform gas-rich sub-Neptunes into smaller rocky worlds and can create enormous hydrogen or helium tails around close-in planets.
* Atmospheric retention depends on the combined effects of gravity, temperature, composition, stellar radiation, stellar wind, magnetic environment, atmospheric replenishment, and geological history.
* For this reason, atmospheric escape is one of the central processes linking [[Planetary Atmospheres]], [[Space Weather]], [[Planetary Evolution]], [[Water in the Solar System]], and [[Planetary Habitability]].
