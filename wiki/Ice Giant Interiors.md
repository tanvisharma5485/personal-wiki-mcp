# Ice Giant Interiors

The interiors of [[Uranus]] and [[Neptune]] contain proportionally more heavy, volatile-rich material than the hydrogen-helium-dominated gas giants, but their exact structures and material states remain poorly constrained.

## Key Points

* "Ice giant" describes bulk composition and formation history, not a present interior made of ordinary frozen ice. Water, ammonia, and methane incorporated as ices can become dense fluids, ionic or superionic phases, reactive mixtures, and other exotic states under interior pressures and temperatures.
* A traditional model places a hydrogen-helium envelope above a volatile-rich "icy mantle" and rock-rich core. Modern models allow gradual composition gradients, mixed rock and volatiles, hydrogen at depth, stable layers, and indistinct core-mantle boundaries; neither planet has a conventional solid surface.
* Ionic or superionic water and other high-pressure mixtures may conduct electricity. Dynamos operating in relatively shallow or thin conducting shells could help produce the strongly tilted, offset, multipolar fields described in [[Planetary Magnetism]].
* Uranus emits very little excess internal heat. Stable compositional stratification, suppressed or layered convection, an insulating region, initial conditions, impacts, or mixing history are possible explanations, but none is established.
* Neptune is denser and emits substantially more internal energy than Uranus, helping power vigorous atmospheric activity. Explaining the two planets' sharply different heat transport and thermal evolution is a central open problem.
* Gravity harmonics, magnetic fields, shape, rotation, atmospheric composition, heat emission, equations of state, and high-pressure experiments constrain models. Available gravity and rotation data are much less precise than for Jupiter and Saturn because [[Voyager Program|Voyager 2]] provided only brief flybys.
* Interior inference is degenerate: different proportions and distributions of rock, volatile material, hydrogen, helium, and heat can reproduce similar masses and radii. Future orbiters and atmospheric probes could sharply improve gravity, magnetic, composition, rotation, shape, and heat-flow measurements.
* Uranus and Neptune may have stopped short of runaway gas accretion because of formation timing, location, disk lifetime, growth rate, available gas, migration, or dynamical interactions. Their interiors also provide reference points for interpreting sub-Neptune and Neptune-size [[Exoplanets|exoplanets]].
* Methane-rich material may dissociate and form sinking diamond-like carbon under some modeled conditions, but "diamond rain" has not been directly observed and its planetary importance is uncertain.
