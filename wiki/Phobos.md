# Phobos

Phobos is the larger and innermost of the two natural satellites of [[Mars]]. The other Martian moon is [[Deimos]].

Phobos is a small, irregularly shaped body with a heavily cratered surface. It orbits extremely close to Mars, much closer to its planet than Earth's Moon orbits Earth.

Its orbit is slowly shrinking because of tidal interactions with Mars. Over very long timescales, Phobos is expected either to break apart and contribute to a temporary ring around Mars or eventually collide with the planet.

Phobos is important for understanding the origin of the Martian moon system, tidal evolution, small-body geology, planetary rings, impact processes, and the history of Mars.

## Key Points

* Phobos is the larger and inner moon of [[Mars]].
* It is a small, irregular, dark, heavily cratered body roughly 27 kilometers across at its longest dimension.
* It orbits only about 6,000 kilometers above Mars and completes one revolution in about 7 hours 39 minutes.
* Because Phobos orbits faster than Mars rotates, it rises in the west and sets in the east when viewed from the Martian surface.
* Phobos lies inside Mars's synchronous-orbit radius, causing tidal forces to slowly pull it inward by roughly 1.8 centimeters per year.
* Tens of millions of years from now, it is expected either to break apart through tidal forces and contribute to a Martian ring or eventually collide with Mars.
* Its most famous surface feature is the enormous Stickney crater, and its surface contains a complex network of grooves.
* The moon's origin remains uncertain. Its dark primitive-looking surface resembles some asteroids, but its nearly circular equatorial orbit is easier to explain through formation from a Martian impact-generated debris disk.
* Future sample-return exploration is expected to provide critical evidence about this origin and may also recover ancient pieces of Mars that were blasted onto Phobos by impacts.
* Phobos therefore provides a unique link among [[Mars]], small-body geology, tidal evolution, impact processes, moon formation, and the possible future development of planetary rings.
