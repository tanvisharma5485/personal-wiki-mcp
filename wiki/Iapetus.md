# Iapetus

Iapetus is the third-largest moon of [[Saturn]] and one of the most visually distinctive satellites in the Solar System. It is famous for two extraordinary characteristics: a dramatic difference in brightness between its dark leading hemisphere and bright trailing hemisphere, and a huge mountain ridge running along much of its equator.

With a diameter of about 1,469 kilometers, Iapetus is slightly smaller than [[Rhea]]. It is composed primarily of water ice with a smaller rocky component and has a heavily cratered, ancient surface.

Iapetus also travels much farther from Saturn than most of the planet's other large regular moons. Its distant, inclined orbit and unusual surface provide valuable information about satellite formation, dust transport, impact history, thermal processes, and the evolution of the Saturnian system.

## Key Points

* Iapetus is the third-largest moon of [[Saturn]], with a diameter of approximately 1,469 kilometers.
* It orbits Saturn every 79 days and is tidally locked to the planet.
* The moon is famous for its dramatic two-tone surface. Its leading hemisphere contains the dark region Cassini Regio, while much of its trailing hemisphere and polar terrain is covered by brighter water ice.
* Dark dust originating partly from Phoebe and other distant irregular satellites is thought to accumulate preferentially on Iapetus's leading side. The resulting temperature difference causes water ice to migrate from warmer dark regions toward colder bright regions, greatly amplifying the original color contrast.
* Iapetus also possesses an enormous ancient equatorial ridge rising many kilometers above the surrounding surface. Its origin remains uncertain and may involve ancient rotational deformation or debris from a former ring or satellite.
* Its overall shape preserves evidence that Iapetus once rotated much faster than it does today, providing a fossil record of early tidal and thermal evolution.
* Together with its heavily cratered surface and unusual orbital environment, these characteristics make Iapetus one of the most distinctive natural satellites in the Solar System and an important source of information about [[Tides]], satellite formation, surface evolution, [[Impact Cratering]], and the history of Saturn's moon system.
