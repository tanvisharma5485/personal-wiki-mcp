# Scattered Disc

The Scattered Disc is a dynamically excited population of icy small bodies located beyond [[Neptune]]. Its members typically follow highly eccentric and often significantly inclined orbits that were strongly shaped by gravitational encounters with Neptune during the early evolution of the Solar System.

Scattered Disc objects are part of the broader population of [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]], but they differ from the relatively stable classical [[Kuiper Belt]] because their orbits are much more elongated and remain dynamically connected, directly or indirectly, with Neptune.

The Scattered Disc is important because it preserves evidence of [[Planetary Migration]], giant-planet scattering, and the restructuring of the outer Solar System. It is also considered one of the major source regions for [[Centaurs]] and many short-period [[Comets]].

## Key Points

* The Scattered Disc is a population of icy bodies beyond Neptune with strongly eccentric and often inclined orbits.
* It formed primarily through gravitational interactions between Neptune and the primordial outer Solar System planetesimal disk.
* As Neptune migrated outward, it scattered objects over enormous distances, captured some into resonances, and left others on the unstable or semi-stable trajectories that define the Scattered Disc.
* The population is dynamically connected to Neptune and continuously loses a small number of objects inward.
* These objects can become [[Centaurs]] and later Jupiter-family [[Comets]].
* The Scattered Disc is therefore both a surviving fossil of early giant-planet migration and an active reservoir feeding the modern comet population.
* Its structure provides critical evidence about [[Solar System Formation]], [[Planetary Migration]], [[Orbital Dynamics]], and the evolution of the outer Solar System.
