# Lunar Geology

Lunar geology is the study of the Moon's:

- Surface

- Crust

- Mantle

- Interior

- Rocks

- Minerals

- Impact history

- Volcanic history

- Tectonic features

- Regolith

- Geological evolution

The Moon preserves an exceptionally ancient geological record because it has:

- No active plate tectonics

- No substantial atmosphere

- No liquid-water erosion

- Very low current geological activity

As a result, many features formed billions of years ago remain visible today.

## Key Points

* Lunar geology records more than four billion years of Solar System history.
* The Moon likely formed after a giant impact involving the early Earth.
* Soon afterward, a:
* Lunar magma ocean
* allowed the Moon to differentiate.
* Light plagioclase floated upward and helped form the ancient:
* Anorthositic highland crust.
* Later enormous impacts created:
* Impact basins
* Many near-side basins were subsequently filled by basaltic lava, producing the dark:
* Lunar maria
* The Moon's surface is dominated by:
* Impact craters
* Regolith
* Basalt plains
* Ancient highlands
* Faults
* Rilles
* The Moon experienced extensive volcanism but lacks present Earth-like plate tectonics.
* Its gradual cooling produced:
* Contraction
* Thrust faults
* Lobate scarps
* Moonquakes
* Billions of years of micrometeorite impacts and solar-wind exposure created the:
* Lunar regolith
* and caused:
* Space weathering.
* Modern exploration has also shown that the Moon contains:
* Polar water ice
* Surface hydroxyl
* Water in some volcanic materials
* Lunar samples from Apollo, Luna, and Chang'e missions provide direct evidence about:
* Crust formation
* Volcanism
* Impact history
* Moon formation
* Because geological recycling is limited, the Moon preserves an ancient record that has largely been erased from Earth.
* Understanding lunar geology is therefore central to planetary science, Solar System chronology, future lunar exploration, and studies of the origin of the Earth-Moon system.
