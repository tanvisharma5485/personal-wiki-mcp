# James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope, commonly called JWST or Webb, is a large space-based observatory designed primarily for infrared astronomy. It studies some of the earliest galaxies in the universe, the formation of stars and planetary systems, the evolution of galaxies, objects within the [[Solar System]], and the atmospheres and physical properties of exoplanets.

JWST was developed through an international partnership led by NASA with major contributions from the European Space Agency and the Canadian Space Agency. It launched on December 25, 2021, and began regular scientific observations in 2022.

Unlike many planetary spacecraft, Webb does not travel from planet to planet. It operates near the Sun-Earth L2 region, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth in the direction away from the Sun. From this environment, its large segmented mirror and extremely sensitive infrared instruments can observe faint and distant astronomical objects.

Although Webb is often described as the scientific successor to the Hubble Space Telescope, the two observatories are not identical. Webb is optimized primarily for infrared wavelengths, while Hubble observes extensively in visible and ultraviolet light as well as some near-infrared wavelengths.

## Key Points

* The James Webb Space Telescope is a large infrared space observatory launched on December 25, 2021.
* It operates near the Sun-Earth L2 region approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth and uses a 6.5-meter segmented primary mirror together with a five-layer sunshield.
* Its four major instrument systemsâ€”NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI and FGS/NIRISSâ€”provide imaging and spectroscopy across near- and mid-infrared wavelengths.
* JWST investigates early galaxies, star formation, [[Planet Formation]], exoplanet atmospheres and objects throughout the [[Solar System]].
* Its infrared capabilities allow it to observe highly redshifted distant galaxies, penetrate dusty star-forming regions, analyze planetary atmospheres and investigate cold objects such as [[Comets]] and [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]].
* For planetary science, Webb complements direct exploration missions such as the [[Voyager Program]], [[Cassini-Huygens]] and [[New Horizons]] by providing sensitive remote observations of many different worlds.
* JWST therefore connects cosmology, stellar astronomy, exoplanet science and Solar System exploration within a single observatory and represents one of the most powerful astronomical facilities placed in space.
