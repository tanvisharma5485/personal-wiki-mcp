# Makemake

Makemake is a dwarf planet and one of the largest known [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]] in the outer Solar System. It orbits beyond [[Neptune]] in the region commonly associated with the [[Kuiper Belt]] and is notable for its bright surface, methane-rich ices, extremely low temperatures, and at least one known moon.

Makemake is smaller than [[Pluto]] but large enough for its own gravity to have shaped it into an approximately rounded body. It is one of the five dwarf planets formally recognized by the International Astronomical Union, together with Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, and Eris.

Its surface chemistry and orbital history make it especially useful for studying volatile retention, radiation processing, dwarf-planet evolution, and the composition of the primordial outer Solar System.

## Key Points

* Makemake is a methane-rich dwarf planet in the trans-Neptunian region beyond Neptune. It was discovered in 2005 and became formally recognized as a dwarf planet in 2008.
* It is roughly 1,400 kilometers across and requires about 305 years to orbit the Sun. Its extremely cold, relatively bright surface contains abundant methane ice and radiation-processed organic-rich compounds.
* Unlike Pluto, Makemake does not currently possess a substantial global atmosphere. Its apparent shortage of accessible nitrogen may be one reason for this difference, although localized or seasonal gas may still be possible.
* Makemake has at least one moon, provisionally designated S/2015 (136472) 1 and commonly called MK2. The moon is much darker than Makemake, possibly because its weaker gravity cannot retain bright volatile frost as effectively.
* Makemake probably formed from icy planetesimals in the primordial outer Solar System and later experienced the dynamical effects of Neptune's migration.
* Although its detailed geology and interior remain unknown, it may be differentiated and could potentially have experienced internal water-rich evolution.
* Makemake therefore provides an important comparison with [[Pluto]], [[Haumea]], [[Sedna]], and other [[Trans-Neptunian Objects]] and helps scientists understand volatile retention, dwarf-planet formation, surface chemistry, and the evolution of the [[Kuiper Belt]].
