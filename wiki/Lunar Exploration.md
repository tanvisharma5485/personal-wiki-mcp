# Lunar Exploration

Lunar exploration is the scientific and technological exploration of the [[Moon]] using telescopes, robotic spacecraft, orbiters, impact probes, landers, rovers, sample-return missions, and human expeditions. The Moon was the first world beyond Earth visited by spacecraft and remains one of the most extensively explored objects in the Solar System.

Exploration of the Moon has transformed our understanding of its origin, [[Lunar Geology]], internal structure, impact history, volcanic evolution, surface composition, water resources, and relationship with Earth. Modern lunar exploration increasingly focuses on the polar regions, particularly the south pole, because permanently shadowed craters there can preserve water ice and other volatile materials.

## Key Points

* Lunar exploration has progressed from telescopic observations to robotic spacecraft, human landings, rovers, orbital mapping, and sample-return missions. Luna 2 became the first human-made object to reach the Moon, Luna 9 achieved the first successful soft landing, and Luna 3 revealed the far side.
* NASA's Apollo program placed twelve humans on the lunar surface between 1969 and 1972 and returned approximately 382 kilograms of lunar material. Soviet Luna missions demonstrated robotic sample return and rover exploration.
* Modern missions from multiple nations have mapped the Moon in unprecedented detail. Chandrayaan-1 contributed to the discovery of widespread water-related signatures, LRO mapped the surface and poles, LCROSS directly detected water in a polar impact plume, GRAIL mapped the lunar gravity field, Chang'e 4 achieved the first far-side landing, Chang'e 5 returned relatively young volcanic material, Chang'e 6 returned the first samples from the lunar far side, and Chandrayaan-3 successfully explored the southern high-latitude region.
* Current and future exploration increasingly focuses on the lunar south pole because of its ancient geology, permanently shadowed regions, and potential water-ice resources.
* The Moon remains scientifically important for understanding [[Lunar Geology]], [[Solar System Formation]], planetary evolution, impact history, and the origin of the Earth-Moon system. It is also becoming an important testing ground for technologies and operational experience needed for sustained human exploration beyond Earth.
