# Impact Cratering

Impact cratering is a major surface-forming process and a primary tool for interpreting the relative ages and histories of solid worlds.

## Key Points

* High-speed impactors generate shock waves, excavate depressions, deposit ejecta, and fracture target material.
* Crater form depends on impact energy, gravity, target material, atmosphere, and later modification; rims, ejecta, secondary craters, and basin morphology help identify impact structures.
* Old, airless, geologically quiet surfaces such as much of the Moon and Mercury preserve craters especially well.
* Earth loses or hides many ancient craters through erosion, sedimentation, oceans, volcanism, and plate tectonics; Venus’s atmosphere filters small impactors and its resurfacing removes older structures.
* Crater counting estimates relative surface age, but resurfacing history must be considered.
